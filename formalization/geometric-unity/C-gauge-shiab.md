## C.1 Reconstruction R-C1 (conventions)

Local model: $A\cdot h=h^{-1}Ah+h^{-1}dh$. $d_{A_0}h=dh+[A_0,h]$ for $h\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Ad}P)$, so $A_0\cdot h=A_0+h^{-1}d_{A_0}h$ (L-C1). In (5.9), $\mathrm{Aut}(\varepsilon_2^{-1},\varpi_1):=\varepsilon_2^{-1}\varpi_1\varepsilon_2$. This is the only reading of (5.9) that makes the product associative: the alternative $\varepsilon_2\varpi_1\varepsilon_2^{-1}$ fails associativity unless the $\varepsilon$'s commute. It is also the reading used in (6.13). Write $c(h):=A_0\cdot h-A_0=h^{-1}d_{A_0}h$. This is a 1-cocycle: $c(h_1h_2)=h_2^{-1}c(h_1)h_2+c(h_2)$.

## C.3 The Shiab operator (layers b, c)

**Reconstruction R-S.** $d=14$, $\eta=(+^7,-^7)$, $\mathrm{ad}=\mathfrak u(64,64)\subset\mathrm{Cl}(7,7)\otimes\mathbb C=M_{128}(\mathbb C)$ (Theorem B1). Take $\Phi^1=\sum_me^m\otimes\gamma_m$ and $\Phi^2=\sum_{m<n}e^{mn}\otimes\gamma_m\gamma_n$. These are the Spin(7,7)-invariant soldering elements in $[\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak u]^{\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)}$ and $[\Lambda^2\otimes\mathfrak u]^{\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)}$ (Def. 8.1). The brackets are $[a\wedge b]_C=\sum e^I\wedge e^J\otimes(a_Ib_J-b_Ja_I)$ and $[a\wedge b]_A=i\sum e^I\wedge e^J\otimes(a_Ib_J+b_Ja_I)$, the two products of the draft's appendix. The family is
$$\circledast^{(s_1;s_2,s_3)}_{\varepsilon,c}\xi=[\Phi^1_\varepsilon\wedge*\xi]_{s_1}-\tfrac c2*[\Phi^1_\varepsilon\wedge*[\Phi^2_\varepsilon\wedge*\xi]_{s_3}]_{s_2},\qquad \Phi_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}\Phi\varepsilon .$$
Conventions: $\beta\wedge*\gamma=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$, $\mathrm{vol}=e^0\wedge\dots\wedge e^{13}$, and pairing $\langle a,b\rangle=\int\mathrm{Tr}(a\wedge b)$ for $a\in\Omega^1$, $b\in\Omega^{13}$. The draft's second term carries "$\tfrac{*}{2}$", read as $c=1$ with an outer Hodge star (page render of p. 43). That outer star is forced by degree counting.

**Proposition C4.**
1. *Degrees.* $\circledast:\Omega^2\to\Omega^{13}$, and $\mathrm{Tr}(T\wedge\circledast\xi)$ is a top form (S0). The draft's degree bookkeeping, $d-1=13$ and $1+13=14$, is consistent.
2. *Tilted equivariance.* $\circledast_{\varepsilon h}(h^{-1}\xi h)=h^{-1}\circledast_\varepsilon(\xi)h$ for all $h\in\mathcal H$. This is immediate from $\Phi_{\varepsilon h}=h^{-1}\Phi_\varepsilon h$. Numerically the relative error is $2.8\times10^{-14}$ (S2).
3. *Untilted operators are not gauge covariant.* If $\varepsilon$ is frozen, the Ricci-type term $\xi\mapsto[\Phi^1\wedge*\xi]_C$ is $h$-equivariant iff $h$ is a scalar, i.e. $h\in U(1)=Z(U(64,64))$.
   *Proof.* Equivariance for all $\xi$ forces $[h^{-1}\gamma_mh-\gamma_m,X]=0$ for all $X\in\mathfrak u$, hence for all $X\in\mathfrak u\otimes\mathbb C=\mathfrak{gl}_{128}$. So $h^{-1}\gamma_mh=\gamma_m+z_m$, and $z_m=0$ by taking traces. Then $h$ commutes with $\mathrm{Cl}(7,7)\otimes\mathbb C=M_{128}(\mathbb C)$. ∎ Numerically the relative error is $1.2$ for a random $h$ (S2). This is why the source must tilt by $\varepsilon$.
4. *Invariance of $I_1^B$.* Under R-C1′ every ingredient of (9.4) is $\tau'$-covariant: $T$, $B_\omega$, $F_{B_\omega}$, $d_{B_\omega}T$, $[T,T]$ and $\Phi_\varepsilon$. The Hodge star acts on the form factor and $\mathrm{Tr}$ is Ad-invariant. So $I_1^B$ is $\tau'(\mathcal H)$-invariant. Under the printed $\tau$ it is not, because $T$ shifts by $-2c(h)$ (Prop. C3).

**Theorem C5 (Einstein contraction; Weyl annihilation).** Let $F$ be the spin curvature $F=\sum_{a<b}e^{ab}\otimes\tfrac14R_{abcd}\gamma^c\gamma^d$ of the Levi-Civita connection of a pseudo-Riemannian metric on $Y^{14}$ (any algebraic curvature tensor $R$). In the conventions of R-S:
$$[\Phi^1\wedge*F]_C=-*\bigl(\mathrm{Ric}_{ab}\,e^a\otimes\gamma^b\bigr),\qquad [\Phi^1\wedge*F]_A=0,$$
$$*[\Phi^1\wedge*[\Phi^2\wedge*F]_A]_A=-\mathrm{Scal}\;*(e^a\otimes\gamma_a),$$
while the three other $(s_2,s_3)$ combinations vanish identically. Hence
$$\circledast^{(C;A,A)}_{\varepsilon,c}F_{B_\omega}=-\,\varepsilon^{-1}*\Bigl(\bigl(\mathrm{Ric}-\tfrac c2\,\mathrm{Scal}\,g\bigr)_{ab}e^a\otimes\gamma^b\Bigr)\varepsilon .$$
For $c=1$, the printed coefficient, this is the Einstein tensor of the **14-dimensional** metric $g_\aleph$ on $Y$. The Weyl part of $R$ is annihilated by every member of the family.
*Proof.* (i) $[\gamma_m,\gamma^c\gamma^d]=2(\delta_m^c\gamma^d-\delta_m^d\gamma^c)$ contracts $R_{abcd}$ to Ricci, and $\{\gamma_m,\gamma^c\gamma^d\}$ is grade 3, which is killed by the first Bianchi identity. (ii) $e^{mn}\wedge*e^{ab}=\langle e^{mn},e^{ab}\rangle\mathrm{vol}$, so the inner bracket is $\sum R_{abcd}[\gamma^a\gamma^b,\gamma^c\gamma^d]_{s_3}$. The commutator is grade 2 and antisymmetric under pair exchange, so it vanishes against $R_{abcd}=R_{cdab}$. The anticommutator has grades 0 and 4; grade 4 vanishes by Bianchi and grade 0 gives a multiple of $\mathrm{Scal}$. (iii) The outer bracket of $\gamma_m$ with a scalar is $0$ for $s_2=C$ and $2i\,\mathrm{Scal}\,\gamma_m$ for $s_2=A$. (iv) The Weyl tensor is totally traceless (L-C3). (v) $F_{B_\omega}=\varepsilon^{-1}F_{\nabla^0}\varepsilon$, then apply C4.2. The overall signs and normalisations are those printed by S1: a fit to $x\,\mathrm{Ric}+y\,\mathrm{Scal}\,g$ gives $(x,y)=(-1,0)$ and $(0,-1)$ with relative residuals $2.7\times10^{-18}$ and $2.1\times10^{-16}$. The Weyl images have norm $\le7.4\times10^{-12}$ against $|R|=4.8\times10^{3}$. ∎

So the draft's "Ricci like / $g_{\mu\nu}$ like / Ricci scalar like" annotation of (9.3) is correct under R-S, **but only with mixed products**: a commutator in the first term and $i$-anticommutators in both brackets of the second. With commutators throughout, which is the "Lie-algebraic" reading, the scalar term vanishes identically. Two limits apply. First, the statement concerns the so(7,7)-valued Levi-Civita curvature. On the rest of $\mathfrak u(64,64)$ the phrase "kills off the Weyl curvature" has no meaning. Second, the Einstein tensor obtained is that of $Y^{14}$, not of $X^4$ (gap GU-G14).

## C.4 The first-order field equation (layer c; negative)

**Lemma C6a (self-adjointness criterion).** Let $L=\circledast_\varepsilon\circ d_{B_\omega}:\Omega^1(\mathrm{ad})\to\Omega^{13}(\mathrm{ad})$. In a $B_\omega$-parallel frame the coefficients of $\circledast_\varepsilon$ are parallel: $\nabla^0\Phi=0$ for the soldering forms, $\nabla*=0$, and C4.2. So by L-C2, $L$ is formally self-adjoint for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, meaning $\langle T,L\alpha\rangle=\langle\alpha,LT\rangle$ for compactly supported $T,\alpha$, iff for every covector index $b$ the pointwise form $\beta_b(T,\alpha):=\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(T\wedge\circledast(e^b\wedge\alpha)\bigr)_{\rm top}$ is antisymmetric in $(T,\alpha)\in\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak u$.
*Proof.* Integrate $\langle T,L\alpha\rangle=\sum_b\int\beta_b(T,\nabla_b\alpha)=-\sum_b\int\beta_b(\nabla_bT,\alpha)$ by parts. At a point the values $\nabla_bT$ ($b=0..13$) and $\alpha$ are independent. ∎ Sanity check: for Chern–Simons ($d=3$, $\circledast=\mathrm{Id}$) the defect is exactly $0$ (S3).

**Proposition C6 (the printed EL equation is not the EL equation of the printed action).** For the Einstein-reproducing member $\circledast^{(C;A,A)}_c$ and every $c\in\mathbb R$, let $T=\gamma_0\gamma_1\,e^0$ and $\alpha=\gamma_0\,e^1$, both in $\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak{so}(7,7)\oplus\mathbb R^{7,7}\subset\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak u(64,64)$. Then
$$\beta_0(T,\alpha)=256,\qquad \beta_0(\alpha,T)=0 .$$
Hence $L\neq L^\dagger$. The part of $\delta_\varpi I_1^B$ that is linear in $T$ and first order is $\tfrac12(L+L^\dagger)T$. Changing the coefficient $k$ of $d_BT$ in the action gives $k(L+L^\dagger)T$, which never equals the $LT$ contained in $\circledast F_{A_\omega}$ ($F_{A_\omega}=F_{B_\omega}+d_{B_\omega}T+\tfrac12[T\wedge T]$). The other terms are zeroth order or $T$-independent, so they cannot compensate. Therefore (9.7)–(9.9) are not the Euler–Lagrange equations of (9.4) for this member. The same holds numerically for every member of R-S: all six term/bracket variants and the full operator at the least-squares optimal $c$ have antisymmetry defects of order $1$ (S3: maximal relative antisymmetry defects $1.56$–$1.92$ over the $\mathfrak u(64,64)$ and grade-1+2 sectors, and $0.94$ at the best $c$). S3 tests the full operator only for $(C;A,A)$. **All eight bracket choices** $(s_1;s_2,s_3)\in\{C,A\}^3$ are covered by `computation/geometric-unity/C_rs_all_members.py` → `.out` (added 2026-10-30, referee GU-5):
- *exact:* for each choice there are Clifford monomials $X,Y$ (times $i$ where needed) with $T=Xe^0$, $\alpha=Ye^1$, and $\beta_0(T,\alpha)=\pm256$ for every $c$, while $\beta_0(\alpha,T)=0$;
- *numerical:* relative defects $0.84$–$1.00$ at the least-squares complex $c$, on two random $\mathfrak u(64,64)$ samples.

For $(C;s_2,s_3)\neq(C;A,A)$ the Einstein-ratio argument of D3.3 already suffices, since the curvature output is $-\mathrm{Ric}$ with no scalar term. For $(A;A,A)$ it suffices when $c\neq0$, since the output is $\tfrac c2\mathrm{Scal}$ with no Ricci term. The remaining cases, $(A;A,A)$ at $c=0$ and $(A;C,C)$, $(A;C,A)$, $(A;A,C)$, need the computation.
*Proof of the displayed values.* $e^0\wedge T=0$, so $\beta_0(\alpha,T)=0$ for any $\circledast$. For $\beta_0(T,\alpha)$, with $\xi=e^{01}\otimes\gamma_0$: the term-1 pairing reduces to $\eta^{00}\eta^{11}\mathrm{Tr}(\gamma_0\gamma_1[\gamma_1,\gamma_0])=2\cdot128$. In term 2 the inner bracket is $i\{\gamma_0\gamma_1,\gamma_0\}=0$. ∎ (The value 256 is printed for $c\in\{0,\pm1,2\}$ in S3.)

**Proposition C6′ (cubic term).** Let $\tau(T_1,T_2,T_3)=\mathrm{Tr}(T_1\wedge\circledast[T_2\wedge T_3]_C)$. Getting $\langle\alpha,k\,\circledast[T\wedge T]\rangle$ from the variation of $\tfrac13\tau(T,T,T)$ requires $\tau(T,T,\alpha)=\kappa\,\tau(\alpha,T,T)$ with $\kappa$ constant. For the members tested, (C;A,A) and (C;C,C), the ratio varies in sign and size across random samples (S4). For Chern–Simons it is identically 1. Even with $\circledast=\mathrm{Id}$, the printed coefficient $\tfrac13$ matches $F$'s $\tfrac12[T\wedge T]$ only if "$[T,T]$" denotes $T\wedge T=\tfrac12[T\wedge T]_C$.

## C.5 What is missing for well-posedness (gap summary)

1. The Shiab operator is a family, not an operator. Choices left open: $\Phi$ (Def. 8.1 allows, e.g., $e^m\otimes\gamma_m\Gamma$), the brackets and $c$; the author's choice is lost (fn. 10). Gap GU-G10.
2. The sign of the tilt is inconsistent (C3), with a one-sign repair (C3′). Gap GU-G11.
3. Under R-S the first-order equation is not variational in the claimed form (C6). Either the action or the equation must change; one candidate is replacing $\circledast\circ d_B$ by $\tfrac12(L+L^\dagger)$. Gap GU-G13.
4. Not written down: the $\varepsilon$-variation ($\Omega^{d}(\mathrm{ad})$ component of (9.5)), the redundancy $\Xi=\mathfrak D\Upsilon$ (9.6), and the variation with respect to $\gimel$, on which the Hodge star, $\nabla^0$ and $\Phi$ depend through $g_\aleph$. Gaps GU-G12, G15.
5. The 14→4 reduction: the Einstein tensor in C5 belongs to $Y$; nothing derives the 4D Einstein equation (9.10). Gap GU-G14.
6. (9.11)–(9.15) and (12.3): the statement that "the second-order equations are automatically satisfied if the first-order ones are" holds **trivially** for any $I_2=\langle\Upsilon,\Upsilon\rangle$, because $\delta I_2=2\langle\Upsilon,\delta\Upsilon\rangle$. It does not depend on $\Upsilon$ being an EL equation. Since $\mathrm{Tr}$ on $\mathfrak u(64,64)$ is indefinite, $\Upsilon=0$ is a critical point but not a minimum.
