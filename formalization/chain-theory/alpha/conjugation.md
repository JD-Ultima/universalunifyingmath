# Far-end conjugation and the direct-sum trace (gaps CT-G03, CT-G04)

**Object.** The identification algebra of a $(d_1,d_2)$ link of the α Letter (Wissner-Gross, v1.0.0, "The charge"; Supplement Lemma S-center, Thm S-stabilizer, Lemma S-conserved; `ChainAlpha.lean` `actingCharge`) and its "acting central charge". Theorem 1 (Uniqueness) uses the latter to exclude 2R 2R 2U at a shared qubit. Readings A, A*, B, C are those of F-CT-3 (`audit.md`).
**Question.** CT-G03: does the axiom fix which block of the identification algebra carries $\bar\xi$, or is the selection invariant? CT-G04: is there an axiom-level origin of the trace on $\mathbb C^{d_1}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_2}$?

It imports `config_enumeration.py` unmodified and checks that its printout equals `config_enumeration.out`. ## Result in brief

1. **The link's operator data do not see the conjugation; the trace does** (Theorem 3).
   - Conjugating the labels at one end ($\varepsilon=(1,-1)$ or $(-1,1)$) leaves every operator on the link unchanged. This covers the image of the identification group, the matchings $U\otimes\bar U$, the stabilizer of the pair ket, the twirl and the reads.
   - It changes only the trace functional, $\operatorname{tr}_1+\operatorname{tr}_2\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_1-\operatorname{tr}_2$.
   - The selection depends on the relabelling only through $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$. The value $+1$ gives the Letter's outcome (the object alone). The value $-1$ gives A*: the object and 2R 2R 2U at a shared qubit.
   - Transposition at every slot (the covariant exchange $E'=E\circ T$) conjugates both ends. It keeps the selection and sends $Y\mapsto-Y$.
2. **Convention-free form** (Props. 1, 2). Let $W=H_1\oplus H_2$.
   - The reading-A link $H_1\otimes H_2$ is the off-diagonal summand of $\Lambda^2W$, and also of $\mathrm{Sym}^2W$.
   - The reading-A* link is the off-diagonal summand $\mathrm{Hom}(H_2,H_1)\cong H_1\otimes\bar H_2$ of $\mathrm{End}\,W$.
   - The centre $\mathcal Q=i(d_2\mathbb 1,-d_1\mathbb 1)$ of $\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u(d_1)\oplus\mathfrak u(d_2))$ acts on these two summands as $i(d_2-d_1)$ and $i(d_1+d_2)$. At $(2,3)$ they are $(2,3)_{1/6}$ in $\mathbf{10}=\Lambda^2\mathbf5$ and $(2,\bar3)_{5/6}$ in $\mathbf{24}$.
   - Reading B takes the trace on $L$ itself. Its centre maps into the centre of $\mathfrak{su}(L)$, which is $0$, under every conjugation.
3. **Matching closure selects A** (Theorem 4).
   - The infinitesimal transpose identity puts the matching algebra $\mathfrak m_0=\{(\zeta,\bar\zeta\oplus0)\}$ in the stabilizer of the pair ket.
   - Every invariant trace condition has the form $\alpha\operatorname{tr}X_1+\beta\operatorname{tr}X_2=0$. Among these, $\mathfrak m_0$ lies in the kernel iff $\alpha=\beta$. That condition is the trace on $H_1\oplus H_2$ of the same end operators that act on the link.
   - A* ($\alpha=-\beta$ in operator form) does not contain the matchings. B ($[d_2:d_1]$) does not contain them at unequal dimensions.
   - In the Letter's labels, the matchings lie in $\mathfrak h_W$ iff $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$.
4. **The direct-sum trace without the direct sum** (Theorem 5).
   - The Lie algebra generated by $\mathfrak{su}(H_1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(H_2)$ and $\mathfrak m_0$ is $\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u(d_1)\oplus\mathfrak u(d_2))$, at every type.
   - The group generated by $SU(H_1)\times SU(H_2)$ and the matchings is $S(U(d_1)\times U(d_2))$.
   - On $L$ alone, the group generated by $SU(H_1)\otimes SU(H_2)$ and the operators $U\otimes(\bar U\oplus1)$ contains the global phases iff $d_1\neq d_2$.
5. **Classification** (Theorem 8, Cor. 9).
   - Take a trace or determinant on any space built from $H_1,H_2$ by $\oplus$, $\otimes$, conjugation (duals), $\Lambda^k$, $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ and $\mathrm{End}$. It gives $\alpha_F\operatorname{tr}X_1+\beta_F\operatorname{tr}X_2=0$, with explicit integer polynomials $\alpha_F,\beta_F$. Every rational ratio occurs.
   - The readout depends only on $[\alpha_F:\beta_F]$ at $(2,2)$ and $(3,2)$.
   - Theorem 1 selects the object alone iff $\alpha=\beta\neq0$ at $(2,2)$ and, at $(3,2)$, $[\alpha:\beta]\neq[2:3]$ (centre-end coefficient first) or there is no condition. In particular, uniqueness *implies* matching closure at $(2,2)$.
6. **What is left.** Under matching closure the only choice left is at $(2,2)$:
   - the proper subalgebra $\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(2))$, in which the global phase of the link is not an identification; this gives the object alone;
   - or all of $\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(2)$ (reading C); this gives the object and 2R 2R 2U, with $1/\text{value}=31.81$.

**Consequence.**
- The far-end conjugation (CT-G03) is not an independent convention once the identification algebra must contain the matchings. Reading A is then forced, and A* is excluded.
- The direct-sum trace (CT-G04) follows from the matchings and the end-local special unitaries. No space $\mathbb C^{d_1}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_2}$ is needed.
- Two clauses remain that the Letter does not state as hypotheses. Both are invariant under every relabelling.
  - **(MC)** A matching is an identification.
  - **(P)** At equal dimensions the global phase of the link is not one. Equivalently, the end-local moves are special unitaries.
- Theorem 1 holds under MC and P. Without MC it fails: A* gives two configurations, and B, which violates MC at $(3,2)$, gives none. Without P it also fails: C gives two configurations.

## 2. Setting

- $H_1,H_2$ are the two ends, of dimensions $d_1,d_2$; $L=H_1\otimes H_2$; $\omega=\sum_{i<m}|ii\rangle/\sqrt m$ with $m=\min(d_1,d_2)$; $S$ is the common support.
- $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak u(H_1)\oplus\mathfrak u(H_2)$ is the space of pairs $X=(X_1,X_2)$ of anti-Hermitian *end operators*. It acts on $L$ by the tensor-product derivation $d\rho(X)=X_1\otimes1+1\otimes X_2$. This action involves no choice. Write $\mathfrak l=d\rho(\mathfrak g)$ (dimension $d_1^2+d_2^2-1$, containing $i1_L$) and $\mathfrak l_0=d\rho(\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su})=\mathfrak l\cap\mathfrak{su}(L)$.
- For $[\alpha:\beta]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb R)$ put $\mathfrak h_{[\alpha:\beta]}=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\alpha\operatorname{tr}X_1+\beta\operatorname{tr}X_2=0\}$.
- *Acting central charge* of a subalgebra $\mathfrak h\supseteq\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su}$: $d\rho(\mathfrak z(\mathfrak h))\neq0$. Equivalently, $i1_L\in d\rho(\mathfrak h)$.
- Since $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak z$ with $\dim\mathfrak z=2$, every subspace $\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su}\subseteq\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak g$ is a subalgebra. It is $\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su}$ itself, some $\mathfrak h_{[\alpha:\beta]}$ (whose centre is the line $\alpha d_1x+\beta d_2y=0$ of $\mathfrak z$), or $\mathfrak g$. The Letter's algebra has a $\mathfrak u(1)$, so it is one of the last two kinds.

The four readings of F-CT-3, as subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$:

| reading | as in F-CT-3 | as a subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ |
|---|---|---|
| A | $\mathfrak h_W=\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u(d_1)\oplus\mathfrak u(d_2))$, far block acting by $\xi_2$ | $\mathfrak h_+=\mathfrak h_{[1:1]}$ |

## 3. Convention-free form

**Proposition 1 (block decompositions; Proven, out §1).** Let $G=U(H_1)\times U(H_2)\subset U(W)$, block-diagonal, with $W=H_1\oplus H_2$.
1. $\Lambda^2W|_G=\Lambda^2H_1\oplus(H_1\otimes H_2)\oplus\Lambda^2H_2$ and $\mathrm{Sym}^2W|_G=\mathrm{Sym}^2H_1\oplus(H_1\otimes H_2)\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^2H_2$. On the middle summand, $e_a\wedge f_b\mapsto e_a\otimes f_b$ (respectively $e_af_b\mapsto e_a\otimes f_b$) intertwines with $d\rho$: the reading-A link.
2. $\mathrm{End}\,W|_G=\mathrm{End}\,H_1\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(H_2,H_1)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(H_1,H_2)\oplus\mathrm{End}\,H_2$ under $\mathrm{ad}$. On $\mathrm{Hom}(H_2,H_1)$, row-major vectorization gives $X_1\otimes1+1\otimes\bar X_2$: the reading-A* link, $H_1\otimes\bar H_2\cong H_1\otimes H_2^*$. On $\mathrm{Hom}(H_1,H_2)$ it gives $X_2\otimes1+1\otimes\bar X_1$.
3. $\mathcal Q=i(d_2 1_{d_1},-d_1 1_{d_2})$ acts as $i$ times a scalar on each summand:

   | $\Lambda^2H_1$, $\mathrm{Sym}^2H_1$ | $H_1\otimes H_2$ | $\Lambda^2H_2$, $\mathrm{Sym}^2H_2$ | $\mathrm{Hom}(H_2,H_1)$ | $\mathrm{Hom}(H_1,H_2)$ | $\mathrm{End}\,H_i$ |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | $2d_2$ | $d_2-d_1$ | $-2d_1$ | $d_1+d_2$ | $-(d_1+d_2)$ | $0$ |

   So the A link acts iff $d_1\neq d_2$, and the A* link at every type.
4. At $(2,3)$, with $Y=\mathcal Q/6i=\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac12,\tfrac12;-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$ and $SU(2)\times SU(3)$ labels: $\Lambda^2W=\mathbf{10}=(1,1)_1+(2,3)_{1/6}+(1,\bar3)_{-2/3}$; $\mathrm{Sym}^2W=\mathbf{15}=(3,1)_1+(2,3)_{1/6}+(1,6)_{-2/3}$; the off-diagonal part of $\mathrm{End}\,W=\mathbf{24}+\mathbf1$ is $(2,\bar3)_{5/6}+(2,3)_{-5/6}$.

*Proof.* Direct, from $X(e_a\wedge f_b)=X_1e_a\wedge f_b+e_a\wedge X_2f_b$, from the same rule for $\mathrm{Sym}^2$, and from $[X,E_{a,d_1+b}]=X_1E_{a,d_1+b}-E_{a,d_1+b}X_2$ with $\mathrm{vec}(X_1Y-YX_2)=(X_1\otimes1-1\otimes X_2^{\mathsf T})\mathrm{vec}\,Y$ and $-X_2^{\mathsf T}=\bar X_2$ on $\mathfrak u(d_2)$. The script checks the block identities and their invariance on a basis of $\mathfrak g$ at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3),(2,4),(3,4)$. It checks the charge table there against the closed forms (out §1). ∎

**Proposition 2 (reading B; Proven, out §1).** $\mathfrak h_B=\{X:\operatorname{tr}_L d\rho_\varepsilon(X)=0\}=d\rho_\varepsilon^{-1}(\mathfrak{su}(L))$. For every $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^2$ (Sec. 4) and every type, $\operatorname{tr}_L$ of the centre element $(ix1,iy1)$ is $d_1d_2$ times its scalar on $L$. So the centre of $\mathfrak h_B$ acts on $L$ as $0$. In the language of Prop. 1, the trace space of B is $L$ itself: the link is the whole defining representation of $SU(L)$, not an off-diagonal block, and $\mathfrak{su}(L)$ has no centre. ∎

## 4. Relabellings

Let $c$ be entrywise complex conjugation in the basis of $\omega$. Put $\theta_\varepsilon=(c^{(1-\varepsilon_1)/2},c^{(1-\varepsilon_2)/2})$ on the label group $G=U(d_1)\times U(d_2)$, and $\rho_\varepsilon=\rho\circ\theta_\varepsilon$, so that $d\rho_\varepsilon(X)=X_1^{(\varepsilon_1)}\otimes1+1\otimes X_2^{(\varepsilon_2)}$. Reading A is $(\rho_{(1,1)},\mathfrak h_W)$; reading A* is $(\rho_{(1,-1)},\mathfrak h_W)$.

**Theorem 3 (what a relabelling fixes and what it moves; Proven, out §3; Lean `actEps_zero_iff`, `actEps_product`, `relabellings`).**
1. For all four $\varepsilon$: $\rho_\varepsilon(G)=U(H_1)\otimes U(H_2)$, $d\rho_\varepsilon(\mathfrak g)=\mathfrak l$ and $d\rho_\varepsilon(\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su})=\mathfrak l_0$. The dimensions at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3)$ are $7,12,17$ and $6,11,16$.
2. For every $S\subseteq U(L)$, $\rho_\varepsilon^{-1}(S)=\theta_\varepsilon^{-1}(\rho^{-1}(S))$.
   - So every subgroup of $G$ defined from $(\rho,\omega)$ alone is carried by $\theta_\varepsilon^{-1}$, and its image in $U(L)$ does not depend on $\varepsilon$. Examples are the labelled matchings, $\rho^{-1}(\mathrm{Stab}\,\omega)$, $\ker\rho$ and $\rho^{-1}(SU(L))$.
   - In labels the matchings are $\{(\zeta^{(\varepsilon_1)},(\bar\zeta\oplus0)^{(\varepsilon_2)})\}$, and they act on $L$ as the same operators for every $\varepsilon$.
   - The twirl, the commutant $\{\mathbb 1,P_m\}$, the transpose identity and the reads are statements about operators on $L$, so they do not involve $\varepsilon$.
3. $\theta_\varepsilon^*(\operatorname{tr}_1+\operatorname{tr}_2)=\varepsilon_1\operatorname{tr}_1+\varepsilon_2\operatorname{tr}_2$, so $\theta_\varepsilon^{-1}(\mathfrak h_W)=\mathfrak h_W$ iff $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$.
4. Through $\rho_\varepsilon$, $\mathcal Q$ acts on $L$ as $i(\varepsilon_1d_2-\varepsilon_2d_1)$. This vanishes iff $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$ and $d_1=d_2$, and also iff $i1_L\notin d\rho_\varepsilon(\mathfrak h_W)$. So the acting predicate, and with it Theorem 1's selection, depends on $\varepsilon$ only through $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$:

   | $\varepsilon$ | $\mathcal Q$ on $L$ at (2,2), (2,3), (3,3) | selected |
   |---|---|---|
   | $(1,1)$, $(-1,-1)$ | $0,\ \pm1,\ 0$ | the object |
   | $(1,-1)$, $(-1,1)$ | $\pm4,\ \pm5,\ \pm6$ | the object and 2R 2R 2U at a shared qubit |

5. Hence the only input of the selection that is not invariant under a one-end relabelling is the trace functional evaluated on the labels. The operator data of the link are invariant under all four relabellings; the selection is invariant exactly under those with $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2=1$.

*Proof.* (1) $\theta_\varepsilon$ is an automorphism of $G$ that preserves $SU(d_1)\times SU(d_2)$; the ranks are computed in out §3(a). (2) is the definition, plus $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta_\varepsilon^{-1}(g))=\rho(g)$. (3) $\operatorname{tr}\bar X=\overline{\operatorname{tr}X}=-\operatorname{tr}X$ on $\mathfrak u(d)$. (4) $d\rho_\varepsilon(ix1,iy1)=i(\varepsilon_1x+\varepsilon_2y)1$, evaluated at $(x,y)=(d_2,-d_1)$. Since $\mathfrak l_0$ contains no nonzero scalar, $d\rho_\varepsilon(\mathfrak h_W)=\mathfrak l_0+\mathbb R\,d\rho_\varepsilon(\mathcal Q)$. The table is out §3(c). (5) follows from (1)–(4). ∎

*Remarks.*
- (a) **Qubit.** On $SU(2)$, $c$ is inner: $\sigma_y\bar U\sigma_y=U$. On $U(2)$, $\sigma_y\overline{e^{i\varphi}U}\sigma_y=e^{-2i\varphi}(e^{i\varphi}U)$. So $c$ inverts the central $U(1)$, and is not inner (out §3(d)). At $(2,2)$, $\theta_{(1,-1)}$ is inner on $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ and acts on the centre by $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$. The whole difference between A and A* at $(2,2)$ is therefore in the $\mathfrak u(1)$.
- (b) **Qutrit.** On $SU(d)$, $d\ge3$, $c$ is outer: $U=\mathrm{diag}(i,i,-1)$ has $\operatorname{tr}U=-1+2i\neq\operatorname{tr}\bar U$ (out §3(d)). By (1) it does not change the image of $\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su}$ on $L$; it only relabels $3\leftrightarrow\bar3$. The selection is decided on the centre alone.
- (c) **Transposition.** $(X_1\otimes1+1\otimes X_2)^{\mathsf T}=-(\bar X_1\otimes1+1\otimes\bar X_2)$ (out §3(e)), so transposing both ends of a link is the diagonal relabelling $\varepsilon=(-1,-1)$. The covariant exchange $E'=E\circ T$ (F-CT-10) identifies the two read links by transposition at every slot. It is therefore a diagonal relabelling: it keeps the selection and sends $Y\mapsto-Y$. It does not produce A*.
- (d) **Completeness of the relabellings** (standard; not computed). For $d\ge2$, every automorphism of $U(d)$ is inner or inner composed with $c$. An automorphism preserves $SU(d)$ and the central $U(1)$; for $d\ge3$, conjugation on $SU(d)$ and the identity on the centre (or the reverse) are incompatible on $\mu_d$. For $d=2$ the extra map $g\mapsto\det(g)^{-1}g$ equals $\mathrm{Ad}(\sigma_y)\circ c$. Inner automorphisms are basis changes, and they preserve every trace condition. Up to basis changes, therefore, the $\theta_\varepsilon$ are all relabellings that act end by end.
- (e) **Hypercharge.** The values $1/6$ and $5/6$ are relative to a trace space ($W$, or $W'=H_1\oplus\bar H_2$). On $L$ alone $\mathcal Q$ is a scalar. The readout $\operatorname{tr}_RM/\dim R$ of Theorem 2 involves neither $\mathfrak h$ nor $\mathcal Q$. Under A* the unit $B(\mathcal Q,\mathcal Q)=1$ would need $\mathcal Q/5$, and $\mathcal Q$ is fixed only up to scale. The value is the same under every reading.

## 5. Matching closure and generation

**Theorem 4 (matching closure; Proven, out §4(a), (c); Lean `mc_iff`, `mc_eps`).** Let $d_1=m\le d_2$.
1. For $\zeta\in\mathfrak u(m)$ the transpose identity gives $(\zeta\otimes1)\omega=(1\otimes\zeta^{\mathsf T})\omega$, and $-\zeta^{\mathsf T}=\bar\zeta$. So $\mathfrak m_0\subseteq\mathrm{stab}_{\mathfrak g}(\omega)$.
   - The full stabilizer is $\{(\zeta,\bar\zeta\oplus c):c\in\mathfrak u(d_2-m)\}$, of dimension $d_1^2+(d_2-d_1)^2$: 4, 5, 9, 8, 10 at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3),(2,4),(3,4)$.
2. $\mathfrak m_0\subseteq\mathfrak h_{[\alpha:\beta]}$ iff $\alpha=\beta$.
3. In $\varepsilon$-labels, $\theta_\varepsilon^{-1}(\mathfrak m_0)\subseteq\mathfrak h_W$ iff $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$.
4. So MC holds as follows for the four readings:
   - A: at every type.
   - A*: at no type.
   - B: only at $d_1=d_2$, where $\mathfrak h_B=\mathfrak h_+$.
   - C: trivially, since it imposes no condition.

*Proof.*
1. Compare components. The general stabilizer element is obtained by solving $d\rho(X)\omega=0$ (out §4(a)).
2. On $\mathfrak{su}(m)\oplus0$ both traces vanish. On $\mathcal U_m$ the condition reads $\alpha(im)+\beta(-im)=im(\alpha-\beta)$.
3. The label traces of $\mathcal U_m$ are $(\varepsilon_1im,-\varepsilon_2im)$.
4. By (2), using the table of Sec. 2.

The script checks (2) on a basis of $\mathfrak m_0$ at five types, and (3) for all four $\varepsilon$ at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3)$. ∎

So MC fixes the variance of the trace space to be that of the link. The trace must be taken on $H_1\oplus H_2$ in the same end operators that act on $H_1\otimes H_2$. A* pairs the link action $\rho$ with the trace on $H_1\oplus\bar H_2$ (Prop. 1(2)). That trace removes the $\mathfrak u(1)$ of the matchings, which act on $W'$ with determinant $(\det U)^2$.

**Theorem 5 (generation; Proven; computed at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3),(2,4),(3,4)$, out §4(b), (e)).** Let $d_1\le d_2$.
1. The Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ generated by $\mathfrak{su}(H_1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(H_2)$ and $\mathfrak m_0$ is $\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u(d_1)\oplus\mathfrak u(d_2))=\mathfrak h_+$.
2. With the full stabilizer in place of $\mathfrak m_0$ it is $\mathfrak h_+$ if $d_1=d_2$ and $\mathfrak g$ if $d_1<d_2$.
3. The subgroup of $U(H_1)\times U(H_2)$ generated by $SU(H_1)\times SU(H_2)$ and the matchings $(U,\bar U\oplus1)$, $U\in U(m)$, is $S(U(d_1)\times U(d_2))$.
4. On $L$: $i1_L\in d\rho(\mathfrak{su}\oplus\mathfrak{su})+d\rho(\mathfrak m_0)$ iff $d_1\ne d_2$. ## 6. Classification of natural trace conditions

**Lemma 7 (Proven, out §2).** $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak{su}(d_1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(d_2)$; its dimension $d_1^2+d_2^2-2$ is checked at five types. Every $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant real linear functional on $\mathfrak g$ is therefore $X\mapsto(\alpha\operatorname{tr}X_1+\beta\operatorname{tr}X_2)/i$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R$. For a finite-dimensional representation $\sigma_F$ of $G$, $\operatorname{tr}_F\circ d\sigma_F$ is invariant, and $\det\sigma_F(g)=\det(g_1)^{\alpha_F}\det(g_2)^{\beta_F}$. The reason: $\det\circ\sigma_F$ is a character of the connected group $G$ with differential $\operatorname{tr}_F\circ d\sigma_F$. So traces and determinants give the same conditions at the Lie-algebra level; a determinant condition can add finitely many components. ∎

**Theorem 8 (classification; Proven, computed in out §2, §5; Lean `select_fixed`, `natural_spaces`, `natural_spaces_mc`).** Let $F$ be built from $H_1,H_2$ by $\oplus$, $\otimes$, conjugation (equivalently duals), $\Lambda^k$, $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ and $\mathrm{End}F=F\otimes\bar F$.
1. **Trace calculus.** The coefficient pair is $(1,0)$ for $H_1$ and $(0,1)$ for $H_2$. Conjugation negates it; $\oplus$ adds; $(\alpha,\beta)_{F\otimes F'}=\dim F'\,(\alpha,\beta)_F+\dim F\,(\alpha,\beta)_{F'}$; $\Lambda^kF$ multiplies by $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ and $\mathrm{Sym}^2F$ by $n+1$, where $n=\dim F$. So $\mathrm{End}F$ has $(0,0)$. The script checks these against the representation matrices on a basis of $\mathfrak g$ at $(2,2),(2,3),(3,3)$, for 17, 17 and 15 spaces (those with $\dim F\le40$).
2. **Every ratio occurs.** $[p:q]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$ is realised by $H_1^{\oplus|p|}\oplus H_2^{\oplus|q|}$, with conjugate summands for negative signs. Functoriality alone does not restrict the condition.
3. **Readout.** Take $H_1$ to be the shared (read) end, of dimension $c$, and $H_2$ the far end, of dimension $e$; use the canonical link action.
   - The centre of $\mathfrak h_{[\alpha:\beta]}$ is spanned by $(\beta e,-\alpha c)$ and acts on $L$ as $i(\beta e-\alpha c)$. With no condition, all of $\mathfrak u(1)^2$ acts.
   - The end rule and the two-point definition leave exactly 2R 2R 2U at a shared qubit and the object (Lean `endRule_twoPoint`; out §5).
   - So Theorem 1 selects the object alone iff, at $(2,2)$, $\alpha=\beta$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\neq0$, and, at $(3,2)$, $2\beta\neq3\alpha$ or $(\alpha,\beta)=0$ (Lean `select_general`, `select_fixed`).
   - For a ratio $[\alpha:\beta]$ fixed across types, the selection is: the object alone iff $\alpha=\beta$; 2R 2R 2U at a shared qubit alone iff $[\alpha:\beta]=[2:3]$ (B's ratio at $(3,2)$); otherwise both. "None" needs a type-dependent ratio, as in B (out §5, over the 80 nonzero integer pairs in $[-4,4]^2$).
4. **Non-canonical links.** A far end of variance $-1$ with $[\alpha:\beta]$ is the canonical link with $[\alpha:-\beta]$ (Theorem 3), so (3) covers it.

**Corollary 9 (what Theorem 1 needs; Proven; Lean `select_object_iff`, `select_mc`, `object_forces_mc`, `readings`).** Consider readings of the form $\mathfrak h_{[\alpha:\beta]}$ at each type, or $\mathfrak g$, with any variance of the link.

## 7. The specific readings asked about

- **"The pair ket is the identity in $V\otimes\bar V=\mathrm{End}\,V$."**
  - At $d_1=d_2=d$, $\omega$ defines an antiunitary $J:H_2\to H_1$, $Jv=\sqrt d\,(1\otimes\langle v|)\omega$. So $H_2\cong\bar H_1$, $L\cong\mathrm{End}\,H_1$, and $\omega\mapsto1/\sqrt d$. Through $J$ the matchings become the diagonal $\{(U,U)\}$.
  - This is the **End picture**: $L$ is the off-diagonal block of $\mathrm{End}(W')$, with $W'=H_1\oplus\bar H_2\cong H_1\oplus H_1$, and the trace on $W'$ is $\mathfrak h_-$, reading A*. The **$\Lambda^2$ picture** puts $L$ in $\Lambda^2W$, $W=H_1\oplus H_2$, with trace $\mathfrak h_+$, reading A (Prop. 1).
  - The two pictures describe the same $L$, $\omega$ and operators (Theorem 3).
  - In the End picture the matchings act on $W'$ as $U\oplus U$, with determinant $(\det U)^2$. In the $\Lambda^2$ picture they act on $W$ as $U\oplus\bar U$, with determinant 1.
  - So "$\omega$ is the identity of End $V$" makes A* *available*. It does not force it, and A* is the picture whose trace loses the matchings' $\mathfrak u(1)$ (Theorem 4).
- **"$V_2^*\to V_1$ or $\bar V_2\to V_1$."** $\omega\in H_1\otimes H_2=\mathrm{Hom}(H_2^*,H_1)$. The Riesz map $H_2^*\cong\bar H_2$ is $U(H_2)$-equivariant, so the two descriptions are the same. Both are the End picture, $W'\cong H_1\oplus H_2^*$, and neither changes the action on $L$.
- **"The far end defined by transposition."** The transpose identity is what puts $(\zeta,\bar\zeta)$ in the stabilizer (Theorem 4(1)). Reading the far end through it is MC, which gives reading A. Transposition at both ends is $\varepsilon=(-1,-1)$ (Remark (c)), which is in the same class as A. Neither gives $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2=-1$.
- **$\sigma_y$ at the qubit** (`supplement.tex:170`; upstream `qubit_absorbs_conjugation`). At a qubit far end, $\sigma_y\bar U\sigma_y=(\det\bar U)U$: a matching undoes the conjugation up to the central phase $\det\bar U$. That central phase is exactly what decides the selection (Remark (a)). The identity does not reduce A* to A.

## 9. Consequences for the gaps

- **CT-G03.**
  - **Resolved conditionally on MC.** The far-end conjugation is not an independent convention. All operator data of the link are invariant under conjugation at one end (Theorem 3).
