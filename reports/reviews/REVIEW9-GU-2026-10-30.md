## 1. Items

**R11-1 (error, minor). O5(d)'s "for Rm ≠ 0, $|A|^2$ is unbounded on $S$" is false in indefinite signature; O5(c)'s Z2 step is unstated there.**
- *Location:* O note, O5(c) (l. 73) and O5(d) (l. 74). Also O.3, first bullet ("O5(d) also covers $t,u$ depending on $(x,s)$").
- *What is wrong:*
  1. Take a base of signature (1,3), (2,2) or (2,1), and $R=\omega\otimes\omega$ with $\omega=l\wedge m$, $l$ null, $m\perp l$. This is an algebraic curvature tensor, since $\omega\wedge\omega=0$. It has $\mathrm{Ric}=\langle m,m\rangle\,l\otimes l\neq0$ and $R=0$.
     - All $R_{ij}$ are multiples of one endomorphism $\hat\omega$, with $\sum_{ij}\epsilon_i\epsilon_j\omega_{ij}^2=2|\omega|^2=0$. So $|A|^2=\tfrac{c_V}{4c_H^2}\sum\epsilon_i\epsilon_jG_y(R_{ij}y,R_{ij}y)\equiv0$ on all of $F_x$.
     - `xcheck [31]`, `[32]` check this at random $y$ in (1,3) and (2,2). `[21]` confirms it by brute force in (2,1): the Z2 scalar curvature at off-section points is exactly $s_F$, with $\mathrm{Rm}(x)\neq0$.
     - So the stated reason in O5(d) is false in every indefinite signature, the (7,7) cases included. (In O5(b) the Z2 condition is correctly restricted: "in definite signature: Rm = 0".)
  2. In that example O5(d)'s conclusion survives: the integrand is constant on $S$ and $\int_Sdvol_S=\infty$. For a general curved indefinite base, with $t,u$ depending on $s$, the step from "unbounded" to "the fibre integral over $S$ diverges" is not shown.
     - In Z2 over a definite base it follows from O9 and the triangle inequality on $K$-orbits: $\int_S|c_1-c_2|A|^2|\ge\int|c_1-c_2\langle|A|^2\rangle_K|\,J(a)\,da=\infty$ for $c_2\ne0$, since $\langle|A|^2\rangle_K\propto|\mathrm{Rm}|^2(\mathrm{tr}\,u\,\mathrm{tr}\,u^{-1}-16)$ is unbounded.
     - For flat bases it is the constant case. For Z1 over a curved definite base the $K$-average also contains the uncomputed $q_y$, so the same argument does not close.
     - Nothing downstream uses O5(d): O6, O7 and O10–O15 use flat bases, ray weights or definite signature.
  3. O5(c), Z2: "diverges at every $x$ ($s_F\neq0$)". By (a), the integral is finite only if $s_Y\equiv0$ on $F_x$, i.e. $|A|^2\equiv0$ and $R/c_H+s_F=0$. In indefinite signature $|A|^2\equiv0$ does not force $\mathrm{Rm}=0$ (item 1), so the note needs $R=0$ from $|A|^2\equiv0$. It does not say this.
     - The statement is true for $n=4$. My proof: write $f_B(y)=\sum B^{\alpha\beta}G_y(w_\alpha\cdot y,w_\beta\cdot y)$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathfrak{so}(g)$, so that $|A|^2=f_{B_R}$ with $B_R=\sum\epsilon_i\epsilon_jR_{ij}\otimes R_{ij}$.
       - The kernel of $B\mapsto f_B$ modulo constants is one-dimensional and spanned by the Hodge star (`xcheck [31]`, `[32]`; for $n=3$ it is zero, `[33]`). The injectivity one might expect fails because $u=g^{-1}y$ ranges over $g$-self-adjoint matrices only.
       - So $|A|^2\equiv0$ gives $B_R=c\star$, i.e. $M^2=c\star'$ for $M=\epsilon R$ acting on $\Lambda^2$.
       - Lorentzian case: $\star'$ is a complex structure, and $M$ is $\mathbb C$-linear on $\mathbb C^3$. If $c\neq0$, $M$ is diagonalisable with eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{ci}$, so $\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb C}M$ has nonzero imaginary part, which contradicts Bianchi ($\mathrm{tr}(\star'M)=0$).
       - Cases (2,2) and (4,0): $M=M_+\oplus M_-$ on the 3-dimensional $\Lambda^2_\pm$, and $M_\pm^2=\pm c$ forces $c=0$ by $\det(M_\pm)^2=(\pm c)^3$.
       - Hence $M^2=0$, $\mathrm{tr}\,M=\tfrac12\mathrm{scal}=0$, and $s_Y\equiv s_F\neq0$.
- *Fix:*
  - O5(c):
    - old: "diverges at every $x$ in Z2 ($s_F\neq0$)."
    - new: "diverges at every $x$ in Z2: $s_Y\equiv0$ on $F_x$ would need $|A|^2\equiv0$, which for $n=4$ forces $\sum\epsilon_i\epsilon_jR_{ij}\otimes R_{ij}=0$ (the kernel of $B\mapsto f_B$ is $\mathbb R\star$, and Bianchi excludes $c\star$, $c\neq0$), hence $R(x)=0$, and $s_F\neq0$ (REVIEW9-GU R11-1)."
  - O5(d):
    - old: "$\int_Sdvol_S=\infty$, and for $\mathrm{Rm}\neq0$ $|A|^2$ is unbounded on $S$."
    - new: "$\int_Sdvol_S=\infty$. Proven for flat bases, where the integrand is constant on $S$, and in Z2 for definite bases, where O9 and the triangle inequality on $K$-orbits apply. In indefinite signature $|A|^2$ can vanish identically for $\mathrm{Rm}\neq0$ (e.g. $R=\omega\otimes\omega$ with ω a null simple bivector), and for general curved indefinite bases the divergence is not shown."
  - O.3, first bullet:
