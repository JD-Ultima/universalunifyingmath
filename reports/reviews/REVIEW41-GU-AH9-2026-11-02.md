## Complete quadratic identities

The exact tensors are computed with the accepted T Clifford implementation, including the compact phases. The two endpoint copies are `i c(omega)` at `a=-1/96` and `i w(omega)Gamma` at `a=-3/832`. Their grade-five and grade-nine quadratic outputs are expressed in terms of the Clifford square and vector sandwich of the real five-form.

The coefficient verification is a complete quadratic basis argument. Any pair of coordinate five-blades occupies at most ten of the fourteen axes. Its intersection size, from zero through five, determines its signed-coordinate-permutation orbit. A determinant correction on an unused axis makes the permutation an SO(14) rotation without changing either input; signs are absorbed in independent real symbolic coefficients A and B. All fourteen external slots and all Clifford coefficients are compared as expanded polynomials. The diagonal orbit and polarization cover every square and mixed coefficient of the 2002-dimensional form space.

Thus the certificate establishes all-form tensor identities, rather than inferring them from a few two-term zeros. SO(14) equivariance suffices for the Gamma copy; no assumption about an orientation-reversing convention is required. Additional nondecomposable and full-support forms check implementation details separately.

## The algebraic zero locus

Reversion fixes a real grade-five Clifford element Omega. Its square has only grades zero, four and eight; the sandwich Omega v Omega has only grades one, five and nine. The scalar square component is the positive norm N. Independently contracting the displayed formulas gives the nonzero factors:

| Kernel | Grade-four square factor | Grade-eight square factor |
|---|---:|---:|
| `i c(omega)` | -64 | -592/3 |
| `i w(omega)Gamma` | 1552/3 | 128 |

Hence vanishing of both higher outputs forces Omega²=N. Their nonzero sandwich coefficients then force Omega v Omega to be a vector for every vector v.

For N>0, conjugation by Omega is an orthogonal involution. In an orthonormal eigenbasis, a grade-five monomial commutes precisely with its five included generators and anticommutes with the others. Every nonzero coefficient of Omega must therefore have the same support: the positive eigenspace. There is only one such five-blade. This elementary argument establishes simplicity without importing a general Pin-group classification.

The full sandwich condition is essential. A separate standard-library calculation produced a full-support, nonsimple five-form with scalar square and vanishing weak wedge conditions, but nonzero grade-five sandwich. The public controls retain this negative case. Scalar square or a one-vector wedge relation alone would not prove the proposition.

## Passage to the field equation

AH6 requires a whole constant-odd endpoint domain, not an isolated zero-u point on a moving conic. It gives N>0 and v=Dv=0. AA4 retains all form and chart-weight derivatives: the first copy has derivative grades three/seven, the second seven/eleven. Neither can cancel the quadratic grades five/nine. The curvature and constant baseline contribute only grade one; the even mass equation vanishes in the declared kernel. A full solution therefore lies in the algebraic simple-form locus at every point.

Every nonzero simple five-form has normalized contraction-Gram spectrum consisting of five ones and nine zeros. An open observer domain meets a fibre in a nonempty relatively open patch. AH7 excludes that fixed spectrum on such a patch. This supplies the contradiction for both endpoints, without continuing a field or assuming an analytic base metric.

The opposite pair has derivative grades five/nine and remains outside the argument. Nonzero-u arcs, isolated moving endpoints, coincident kernels, det M=0 and indefinite real forms are also outside the conclusion. Earlier AH6 algebraic norm examples remain valid selected-channel controls; AH9 shows they cannot become full solutions on these two constant endpoint domains.
