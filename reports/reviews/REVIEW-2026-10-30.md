**GU-3 (error, probable). "$w_2(C)=\pi^*w_2(X)$, so the Spin(7,7) structure exists iff $X$ is spin."**
- *Location:* note.tex:43. The same claim is in A-chimeric-signature.md:54 (Prop. A7, "for either choice of $Y$") and reports/geometric-unity-phase1.md:26.
- *Problem (a), non-orientable $X$.* The computation gives $w_1(C)=0$ and $w_2(C)=\pi^*w_2(X)$ for non-orientable $X$ too. For $X=\mathbb{RP}^4$, $w=(1+a)^5=1+a+a^4$, so $w_2=0$, yet $X$ is not spin. The statement should therefore read "iff $w_2(X)=0$".
- *Problem (b), indefinite signature.* For an indefinite metric, lifting the SO(7,7) frame bundle to Spin(7,7) ⊂ Cl(7,7), which (3.32) needs because $\rho_D$ is a representation of Spin(7,7), is obstructed by $w_2(C_+)+w_2(C_-)=w_2(C)+w_1(C_+)w_1(C_-)$ (Karoubi 1968), not by $w_2(C)$.
  - *Sanity check of the criterion.* Take $E=L\oplus L$ with signature (1,1). In Cl(1,1), $(e_1e_2)^2=+1$ and $e_1e_2\mapsto-1\in SO(1,1)$, so the cover splits over the maximal compact. Every orientable (1,1)-bundle therefore lifts, although $w_2(E)=w_1(L)^2$ can be nonzero.
  - *Application to $C$.* Take base (1,3), $\lambda>\tfrac14$ and $c_V,c_H>0$. Then $C_+=V_+\oplus\ell^*$ and $C_-=(\mathbb Rg\oplus\ell^*\!\otimes\Sigma^*)\oplus\Sigma^*$, where $\ell$ is the time line of $y$ and $\Sigma$ its orthogonal complement. Hence $w_1(C_+)=w_1(C_-)=w_1(\ell)$.
  - *Consequence on $Y=\mathrm{Met}(X)$.* The fiber is homotopy equivalent to $\mathbb{RP}^3$ (Prop. A6), and $\ell$ restricts to the tautological line, so $w_1(\ell)^2=a^2\neq0$ in $H^2(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb Z_2)$. The obstruction is nonzero on every fiber: no Spin(7,7) structure exists on $C$ over Met(X), whatever $X$ is.
  - *Consequence on the time-oriented $Y'$* (the draft's (3.33), gu.txt l. 1203). There $\ell$ is canonically oriented, and the condition becomes $w_2(X)=0$.
- *Fix:* redo Prop. A7 with the Karoubi obstruction. State the result for the time-oriented $Y'$ only, and record that Met(X) itself carries no Spin(7,7) structure on $C$. Please check against Karoubi, *Ann. Sci. ENS* 1 (1968) 161.

## 3. `proofs/latex/howard-note/note.tex`

### Issues

**H-1 (error). The Archimedean no-go is false as stated.**
- *Location:* note.tex:59–64. The same statement is A6 in formalization/howard/arithmetic.md:79.
- *What is wrong:* "Let $S$ be closed under $+$ and under division by positive integers. Then no $\otimes$ on $S$ satisfies LD together with $a\otimes b\ge a$." Counterexamples: $S=\{0\}$, or $S=\mathbb R_{\le0}$ with $\otimes\equiv0$, satisfy both LD and $a\otimes b\ge a$. The proof needs some $a>0$.
- *Fix:* "Let $S\subseteq\mathbb R$ contain a positive element …; no $\otimes$ satisfies LD and $a\otimes b\ge a$ for all positive $a,b$."

**H-2 (error). Most of the lattice's inconsistency certificates are hand proofs, not z3.**
- *Location:* note.tex:95 ("The lattice is decided by z3 (negation UNSAT for each inconsistent subset)"), together with the abstract l. 25 ("each with a certificate") and the title ("machine-checked certificates").
- *What is wrong:* axiom_lattice.out Part 2 shows that the z3 cores decide only 29 of the 58 inconsistent subsets. The other 29 are reported `open`, and are decided only by adding the cores LD+INC and RD+INC "(Thm A6)". That is a hand proof: it appears neither in z3 (the only INC certificate is H1+LD+INC; there is none for RD+INC) nor in HowardArith.lean, which has no Archimedean theorem. "Negation UNSAT" is also the wrong notion: an inconsistent subset is certified by UNSAT of its own ground instances.
- *Evidence:* the referee script `review/howard/lattice_check.py` recounts from the seven stated cores: 58/70, with no conflicts. The five maximal consistent sets equal the maximal core-free sets, each core is minimal, and each witness satisfies its set (checked numerically on random reals).
- *Fix:* "z3 certifies 29; the other 29 follow from Theorem 4 (hand proof)". Or add the ground certificate $1\otimes\tfrac14$ for RD by symmetry, and a Lean proof of the Archimedean step over ℚ.

**H-3 (overclaim). The ring-level no-go contradicts the paper's own example.**
- *Location:* note.tex:27 ("No ring, semiring or near-ring over ordinary addition realises H1 and H2").
- *What is wrong:* the note's own sharpness example, $\mathbb Z/4$ with $2ab$ (note.tex:45, Lean `char4_*`), is an associative ring over ordinary addition mod 4 that realises H1 and H2. The near-ring part is proved only over $\mathbb Z$ (Theorem 2, l. 52).
- *Fix:* "on $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R$ (more generally, in characteristic not dividing 4)", and extend Theorem 2 to $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb R$ explicitly. The same computation works, since $2(2\otimes1)=4\Rightarrow2\otimes1=2$ needs only absence of 2-torsion.

**H-4 (overclaim). Uniqueness of the bi-additive operation, and the isomorphism.**
- *Location:* note.tex:27 ("the unique bi-additive operation with H1 is $a\otimes b=2ab$, … isomorphic to ordinary multiplication").
- *What is wrong:* on ℝ, uniqueness needs a regularity hypothesis. Theorem 3 (l. 56) says so, and Hamel-basis solutions exist otherwise (arithmetic.md:67). On ℤ, the image of $x\mapsto2x$ is $(2\mathbb Z,+,\cdot)$, not ordinary multiplication on ℤ.
- *Fix:* qualify both statements accordingly.

**H-5 (overclaim). "Every consistent reading is a relabelling of standard arithmetic."**
- *Location:* note.tex:27, and the Remark at l. 78 ("Each consistent reading is isomorphic to a standard structure").
- *What is wrong:* nothing in the paper classifies models of the consistent sets. The lattice exhibits one witness per maximal set. Two of those witnesses, $2b$ and $2a$ (for {H1,H2,LD} and {H1,H2,RD}), are neither associative nor commutative, are not relabellings of any standard multiplication, and are not among the four readings in the Remark. The only classification results are A3 (bi-additive) and A7 (continuous, strictly monotone, associative), and each carries hypotheses.
- *Fix:* restrict the claim to those classes, or drop it.

**H-6 (unclear). Which rules the lattice covers.**
- *Location:* note.tex:18–25 and the title, l. 10 ("complete").
- *What is wrong:* the abstract lists four Howard rules, including EFF, and says "we decide every combination of these with distributivity, associativity and commutativity". The $2^7$ lattice, however, is over {H1,H2,LD,RD,AS,CM,INC}. EFF is not in it: the weak form is implied by H1, and the strong form (no identity) is not decided. BAL (arithmetic.md:16) is omitted as well.
- *Fix:* name the seven rules and drop "every combination of these".

**H-7 (minor). Sources and bibliography.**
- *Location:* note.tex:33 and :98.
- *What is wrong:* the JRE #2152 transcript is a third-party machine transcript that was not checked against the audio (sources/howard.md TH-S2). The note should say so. "Lipton and Regan (2018)" is known to the project only from a search summary (sources/howard.md TH-S9: "Search summary only"); prior art should not be attributed to a text that was not read. The note has no bibliography at all. Missing references: the 2015 *Rolling Stone* profile and its CBS reprint; JRE #2152 and #2171; Lipton–Regan; Aczél (Cauchy); Hamel; Pilz (near-rings); Litvinov (Maslov dequantization); Maclagan–Sturmfels.
- *Fix:* add the disclosure, read and cite Lipton–Regan properly or drop the attribution, and add a bibliography.

**H-8 (minor). Axiom audit coverage.**
- *Location:* note.tex:95; HowardArith.lean.out.
- *What is wrong:* the committed `#print axioms` covers 11 of the file's 51 theorems.
- *Evidence:* the referee printed all 51 under Lean 4.35.0 (exit 0, 1.1 s). None uses more than `propext` and `Quot.sound`, so the claim is true. Lean 4.24.0 could not be checked here.
