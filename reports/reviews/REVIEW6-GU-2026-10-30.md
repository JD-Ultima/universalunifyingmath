**R8-5 (presentation). Five wording points in the L note's proofs and setting; no result changes.**
- *(a) L3(a), proof (l. 73):* "$V_S$ commutes with $\mathfrak{so}(\eta)$, which acts irreducibly on $\mathbb R^4$. So $V_S$ is a multiple of $1$."
  - Over $\mathbb R$, irreducibility only makes the commutant a division algebra ($\mathbb R$, $\mathbb C$ or $\mathbb H$). For example, $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ acts irreducibly on $\mathbb R^2$ with commutant $\mathbb C$.
  - What is needed is absolute irreducibility. It holds for every $(p,q)$ with $p+q=4$: the complexification is the vector representation of $\mathfrak{so}(4,\mathbb C)$.
  - The point is not vacuous in indefinite signature: $\mathfrak{so}(2,2)\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)^2$ and $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)_{\mathbb R}$.
  - The code checks the statement actually needed: output §L3, $\{v\in\mathfrak p:[[X,Y],v]=0\}=0$. `xcheck §X3` confirms that the commutant of $\mathfrak{so}(\eta)$ in $\mathfrak{gl}(4)$ is $\mathbb R1$ in (4,0), (1,3), (3,1) and (2,2).
  - Fix:
    - old: "which acts irreducibly on $\mathbb R^4$. So $V_S$ is a multiple of $1$"
    - new: "whose commutant in $\mathfrak{gl}(4)$ is $\mathbb R1$ ($\mathbb R^4$ is absolutely irreducible: its complexification is the vector representation of $\mathfrak{so}(4,\mathbb C)$; output §L3). So $V_S$ is a multiple of $1$"

**My derivation of (C1).**
- Write $\hat{\mathcal R}=g_F^{-1}(\mathrm{Ric}_F+\mathcal A)$. Differentiate $\nabla_Z\nabla w=\mu(w)\hat{\mathcal R}Z$ and use $\nabla\mathrm{Ric}_F=0$. This gives the note's identity
