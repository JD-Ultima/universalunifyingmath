## The universal symmetric tensor

Both single Lambda-five branches retain the true dualized equation, real compact $(14,0)$, $\mathfrak u(128)$, fixed parallel real $W_A$, constant nonzero $\kappa$, invariant odd field and both vector-block invertibility conditions. All other non-vector singular factors are excluded. The compact phases differ: $i$ for the grade-four/eight pair, and $1$ for the grade-six/ten pair. The helper checks the actual polarization and phases for both, rather than substituting only a block template.

The all-form identity follows from a complete contraction count. Two alternating five-form inputs and two symmetric output indices give twelve slots. A metric pairing within either alternating input vanishes. Pairing the output indices together gives $|\omega|^2 I$; pairing one output index to each input gives its contraction Gram tensor $S$. Pairing both outputs into one input leaves an unavoidable trace in the other. An epsilon tensor cannot occur below fourteen slots. Thus there are exactly two possible patterns, including when the five-form is not simple.

The complete symbolic-weight output on $e^{01234}$ has eigenvalues $-48rs$ and $160rs/3$ on dimensions five and nine. Its two independent patterns fix both universal coefficients:

$$\operatorname{Sym}([Q(k)]_{13}\Gamma/i)
=\frac{160}{3}rs|\omega|^2I-\frac{304}{3}rsS.$$

Right multiplication by $\Gamma$, rather than its inverse, is essential to these signs. General nondecomposable, off-diagonal-Gram and full-support controls separately agree with the formula; the universal statement comes from the contraction proof, not their finite number.

Neither the derivative nor the vector/kernel mixed term can contribute a symmetric converted tensor: their input is $V\otimes\Lambda^5=\Lambda^6\oplus\Lambda^4\oplus H_5$, which has no symmetric two-tensor summand. This applies to arbitrary covariant form jets and weight derivatives. It does not borrow AA4's derivative factorization for the opposite pair. The other derivative components remain equations. Together with AF1's invariant/vector terms and absence of original grade thirteen in the curvature term, this gives the displayed full symmetric equation without assuming the slaved baseline solves it.

## Positivity, rank and branch signs

At $w\ne0$, both weights are nonzero. If $\omega=0$, the symmetric equation reduces to a scalar identity equal to a tensor of rank at most one. Hence $C=0$ and $a=-1/208$, but both Lambda-five conics have negative $f$ there. This correctly establishes $N=|\omega|^2>0$ before the Gram-rank argument.

Dividing only by nonzero $rs$ gives

$$S=\alpha I-bC\otimes C,\qquad b=\frac{624}{19}\frac{w}{rs}.$$

The original positive metric is used here. Since $S=L^*L$ for $L(X)=\iota_X\omega$, its kernel is the form's annihilator and its rank is the dimension of the smallest covector support. A nonzero five-form has rank at least five. On $C^\perp$, its eigenvalue is $\alpha$; positivity and the rank bound force $\alpha>0$. Thus only ranks thirteen and fourteen remain. This argument would not hold with an indefinite pairing.

The trace $\operatorname{tr}S=5N$ gives the two eigenvalues

$$\frac{5N+b|C|^2}{14},\qquad
\frac{5N-13b|C|^2}{14}.$$

The kernel equations imply $rs/w=ps^2/(1+da)$. Therefore $b<0$ on the left branch: both eigenvalues are positive and $|b||C|^2<5N$. On the right $b>0$, positivity gives $b|C|^2\le5N/13$. Equality is exactly the rank-thirteen case, with $C\ne0$ and annihilator line $\mathbb RC^\sharp$; otherwise the rank is fourteen. For $C=0$, the Gram tensor is positive isotropic and has full rank. No sign restriction on $\kappa$ was introduced.

Consequently support dimension at most twelve, including simple five-forms, is excluded at nonzero $w$. The argument does not classify the allowed forms or exclude every Lambda-five field. Zero-$w$ endpoints are not subject to a division by $rs$ and remain outside this conclusion.
