# Review86. A common positive receiver on the full triangular space

**Date** 2026-11-14. **Verdict** Accept BV1–BV3 for the declared
triangular closed family.
[Proof](../../formalization/geometric-unity/BV-h6-triangular-positive-obstruction.md)
and [verifier](../../computation/geometric-unity/BV_h6_an_positive.py).

The receiver's exact sign invariance makes all mixed character pairings
zero. Trace removal verifies that its pairing depends only on homogeneous
necessary equations. The basis columns belong to exact closure stacks,
and strictly positive Gram matrices on them imply their independence.
Because their counts equal the exact sign-block dimensions, this applies
to the entire closed family.

The primary verification converts rational field columns to controlled
dyadic approximations, computes the contracted receiver mod six primes,
and reconstructs the integer matrix within an explicit size bound.
Bareiss pivots verify the leading minors after subtracting the uniform
margin. The four-bit Pauli splitting retains the complete operator and
the exact Clifford trace pairing. All principal calculations share
the stated observer, quadratic formula and coefficient conventions.

An independent closure and square-root interval check uses all original
stack rows, a finer dyadic enclosure and the sum of individual error
squares. It agrees with the supplied error allowance in every block.
A separate exact 128-dimensional Clifford calculation uses short
integer limb products and directly extracts the receiver pairing. Its
selected character-15 entry agrees with the 32-dimensional CRT result
as a rational number. A separate rational elimination checks the exact
positive matrices in the smallest-margin blocks and additional controls.

The full polynomial extension is bounded on arbitrary dyadic input
tuples. Its homogeneous polarization gives a uniform perturbation
allowance less than $10^{-4}$ in each block, below the exact margin
$1/25$. Therefore the verification does not require the rounded
tuples to be exact hooks. This is an internal computer-assisted
review. External peer review remains outstanding.
