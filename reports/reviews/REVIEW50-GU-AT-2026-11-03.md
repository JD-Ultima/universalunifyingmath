## Scalar equation and bounded germ

The substitution `y=c0*c`, `t=kappa^2*c0` transforms AR5b exactly to
`D y'+P y+t Q+R=0`. The level `a*=-491/26416` is on the admissible left arc,
with both vector-block factors nonzero. The coefficients are analytic near it;
D has a simple zero. Dividing by its nonzero constant derivative gives
`x y'+p(x)y=q(x;t)`, with `p(0)=153/127>0`.

AT1's integrating factor has logarithmic derivative `(p-p0)/x`, with a removable
singularity. For `z=v*y`, the equation is `x z'+p0 z=v*q`. The weighted integral
over `0<u<1` converges because p0>0. Uniform convergence of the analytic series
for vq on a smaller interval justifies termwise integration and coefficients
`b_n/(n+p0)`. This proves convergence, analyticity and the differential equation.
On either punctured side every nonzero homogeneous difference is a constant
times `abs(x)^(-153/127)/v(x)` and is unbounded. Bounded uniqueness therefore
holds separately from either side, without a global foliation assumption.

The exact y0 and y1 agree with the equation. For
`tau*=3241880613/6380808980`, y0 is positive when `0<t<tau*`, zero at equality
and negative above it. The slope y1 is positive for all t>0. At equality the
scalar germ has a simple zero and only its right side is positive. AT correctly
keeps this one-sided scalar possibility until the actual critical-point proof.

`(Ric+I/c0)A=0`; the term involving the derivative of Ric vanishes. AR0 yields

Ric positive, contradicting its negative Euler direction; a double zero root

`m=rank A in {1,...,14}` and
`eta=-(kappa^2 U(a*)/6+1/c0)/(24+2m)<0`.

The Euler Ricci identity gives `A(E)=eta E`. N1 makes the actual Euler curve

`da=delta*C/24`. Its a-coefficient is positive because delta(a*) and eta are
negative. On a short positive half-curve, a is strictly increasing and every

there. Inverting a(s) therefore gives a one-sided AT0 solution bounded with
limit zero, without assuming profile smoothness at the critical point.
