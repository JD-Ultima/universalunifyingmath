The metric is the fully specified local quadratic polynomial with curvature $\rho R_0+\omega\mathcal W$ at the origin. Its cubic and quartic metric coefficients are zero. ## The polynomial reconstruction is justified

At the origin, the observer metric and inverse are independent of $w$. The base connection has linear term of degree at most one in $w$, zero quadratic term, and cubic term of degree at most two. Thus the observer first derivatives have degree at most one, and its second and third derivatives at most two. The inverse metric introduces no parameter-dependent denominator.

Riemann and Ricci are therefore polynomials of degree at most two. Their covariant derivative has degree at most three, allowing products of three first derivatives. These are bounds on the full tensors before interpolation. Consequently three distinct nodes recover every coefficient entry, and four recover every RHS entry exactly. The fourth-node check of the coefficient matrix is an additional control. The coefficient determinants are computed over $\mathbb Q[w]$ from these entries; their degrees are not inferred from determinant samples.

In the three selected rows, unwanted coefficients vanish at three nodes and hence identically. The common $n$ combination is fixed by the metric term. Treating it as one free scalar yields the necessary three-equation system with columns $(a_i,1,b_i)$. A nonzero compatibility determinant proves inconsistency even under this relaxation. The displayed quadratic RHS polynomials are recovered within the proved cubic bound; their zero cubic coefficients are calculated, rather than assumed.
