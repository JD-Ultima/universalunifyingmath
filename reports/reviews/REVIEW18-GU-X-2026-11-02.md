# Internal review of the symmetric hook family

November 2, 2026. Review of `formalization/geometric-unity/X-symmetric-hook.md`, its symbolic script, output and metric record, following `a0eb818`. This checks the declared family and its pointwise obstruction; it is not external peer review.

## Assessment and family size

X1–X3 are valid as stated. The calculation excludes the declared common-axis symmetric family on the first pure hook branch of the flat-base Z1 model. It does not classify arbitrary hook fields.

For each fixed unit axis $r$, the parameter space is $\operatorname{Sym}^2_0(r^\perp)$, of dimension $13\cdot14/2-1=90$. Its map into $H_2\Gamma$ is injective: the Clifford coefficients of the $B_a$ recover every entry of $S$. Allowing the axis to vary gives a union of these families, rather than one 90-dimensional linear space.

Symmetry cancels the grade-three part of $\sum_a\gamma^a B_a$, and trace zero cancels its grade-one part. Thus the field is in the hook. In signature $(14,0)$, $B_a\Gamma$ is Hermitian and $iB_a\Gamma$ is anti-Hermitian. The normalized positive pairing gives $\|k\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(S^2)$. The compact real form and positive metric are essential to the norm argument.

## The full quadratic identity

The unrestricted diagonal computation uses thirteen independent eigenvalues before imposing trace zero. A single diagonal basis field has zero quadratic term. For two distinct basis fields supported at $b,c\in r^\perp$, I independently checked

$$[i\gamma_b\gamma_r\Gamma,i\gamma_c\gamma_r\Gamma]
=-2\gamma_b\gamma_c.$$

The first differentiated-cubic term consequently contributes $4i\gamma_a$ whenever $a\notin\{b,c\}$. In the adjoint term, the two nonzero contractions each contribute $4i\gamma_a$ for $a\notin\{b,c,r\}$. They vanish at $r$: the relevant bivector commutator fixes the three-plane containing both its indices. Terms at $b,c$ vanish. This recovers X's separate $4/8$ coefficient table for $3Q$, including its signs and factor of three.

Quadratic polarization and permutation symmetry therefore give the polynomial identities

$$Q_a/i=4\sum_{b<c,\ b,c\ne a}\lambda_b\lambda_c,
\qquad Q_r/i=\frac43\sum_{b<c}\lambda_b\lambda_c.$$

The sums become X3 using $L=\sum\lambda_b$ and $N=\sum\lambda_b^2$. At $L=0$, the tensor is

$$Q(k)/i=(4S^2-2NI_{13})\oplus(-2N/3),$$

with zero mixed entries and no grade-five component. Diagonalizing a real symmetric $S$ by an oriented orthogonal basis and using spin equivariance extends this identity to every $S$ in the fixed-axis family. An adapted oriented frame covers every axis. This argument is pointwise and does not differentiate an eigenbasis.

## Scalar obstruction and derivatives

The trace on $r^\perp$ is $-22N$, while the axis component is $-2N/3$. A scalar fourteen-dimensional tensor would require their difference to vanish:

$$\operatorname{tr}_{r^\perp}(Q/i)-13(Q/i)_{rr}=-40N/3.$$

Positivity forces $S=0$. But on flat-base Z1 the required scalar is the negative of

$$\frac{15}{2c_V}+\kappa t+312t^2
=\frac{15}{2c_V}+\frac{201}{1156}\kappa^2>0,$$

so even the zero family member fails. I checked this coefficient and $\det M=(1313/17)\kappa^4>0$ separately. The result holds for either sign of nonzero $\kappa$.

V3's grade argument applies to arbitrary derivatives of a smooth grade-12 field: the spin connection preserves Clifford grade and the parallel volume element. Its differentiated term has grade 11 and cannot cancel this grade-one discrepancy. Allowing $S$ or $r$ to vary does not introduce a grade-one derivative term. The grade-eleven equation has not been solved or removed; the obstruction occurs in a different required equation.
