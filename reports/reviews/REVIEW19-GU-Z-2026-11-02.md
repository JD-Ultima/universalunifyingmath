## Scope

The result covers every smooth Riemannian four-metric having nonzero rank-one Ricci tensor and zero Weyl tensor at the specified base point. Arbitrary cubic and quartic coefficients are allowed; higher smooth terms are irrelevant to the required four-jet. The invariant corollary follows from normal coordinates, rather than assuming that every metric is the special complete polynomial of U.

The full-field consequence retains S.0: compact signature $(14,0)$, Z1 at $\lambda=1/5$, positive $c_H,c_V$, invariant odd field, slaved vector even field, $\kappa\ne0$ and $\det M\ne0$. It does not cover arbitrary even fields or non-vector singular branches.

## Affinity, support and the unchanged contradiction

At the normal base point, the connection's linear term is fixed, its quadratic term is linear in the cubic metric coefficients, and its cubic term is independent of those coefficients and affine in the quartics. In the observer metric, variable cubic contributions first enter the mixed block at order two and the horizontal block at order three. Two variable cubic factors would have order at least four. Thus the observer first jet is fixed, and its second and third jets have exactly the affine dependence asserted in Z1. The curvature formulas introduce no quadratic dependence when the first jet is fixed.

Only mixed metric second derivatives with two base derivative indices vary. Consequently the covariant Riemann variation has three horizontal and one vertical slot. For a diagonal vertical slot, the mixed metric variation is a base gradient; commutation of its third derivatives makes these curvature components zero. The block-diagonal inverse metric, whose vertical block preserves diagonal and off-diagonal subspaces, preserves this support when indices are raised. The Ricci variation therefore has only horizontal/off-diagonal-vertical components.

The three W certificate rows have two horizontal indices and hence even parity under $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$. Reflection reverses every cubic coefficient. Affine dependence forces their cubic RHS variation to vanish; W's independent quartic cancellation supplies the other half. The same support excludes changes in their coefficient rows. This preserves the exact nonzero defects for both signs of $\rho$.

## Why the rank cannot drop

I checked the five invariant row classes separately against the Riemann and Ricci support. Their disjoint counts are $450+64+160+24+24=722$. The exact rank is 38 for both signed representatives. The four supplied kernel vectors are independent and annihilate every row, so rank-nullity proves that they span the entire kernel:

$$v=0,\qquad\xi_V=n_V=0,\qquad
\xi_i=c h_{ii}n^i,\quad c=12/5.$$

For each added row $(i,5,5)$, the Riemann and metric coefficients are fixed. Only the coefficient of $\xi_5$ can change. Its variation therefore annihilates this kernel. On the kernel the row equals

$$-c h_{ii}\left(\widehat{\mathrm{Ric}}^5{}_5+1/c\right)n^i
=-\frac{d_i}{90}n^i.$$

The four values are $-1/45,-1/30,-2/45,-1/18$, all nonzero. These rows eliminate the four remaining kernel directions for every cubic tensor.

The stronger determinant claim also follows. The annihilator of the four-dimensional kernel is exactly the span of the fixed 38 independent rows. Every added-row variation lies in this span; subtracting its combination of those rows restores the baseline added row without changing the determinant. Thus the recorded 42-row coefficient minor is constant and nonzero for arbitrary cubic and quartic coefficients. This step establishes full rank universally; sampled full ranks alone would not do so.

The following complete sign check imports no repository code. It reconstructs the curvature directly from the quadratic metric's second derivatives:

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from itertools import product

p = tuple(F(x, 5) for x in (1, 2, 2, 4))
assert sum(x*x for x in p) == 1
delta = lambda i, j: F(i == j)
for rho in (F(1), F(-2)):
    P = lambda i, j: rho * (p[i]*p[j] - delta(i, j)/6)/2
    R = lambda a, b, c, d: (
        P(a, c)*delta(b, d) + P(b, d)*delta(a, c)
        - P(a, d)*delta(b, c) - P(b, c)*delta(a, d))
    G2 = lambda i, j, k, l: -(R(i, k, j, l) + R(i, l, j, k))/3
    recovered = lambda a, b, c, d: (
        G2(a, d, b, c) + G2(b, c, a, d)
        - G2(b, d, a, c) - G2(a, c, b, d))/2
    assert all(recovered(*ix) == R(*ix)
               for ix in product(range(4), repeat=4))
    ric = [[sum(recovered(a, b, a, d) for a in range(4))
            for d in range(4)] for b in range(4)]
    assert ric == [[rho*p[b]*p[d] for d in range(4)] for b in range(4)]
    print(rho, "all 256 curvature entries and the Ricci sign agree")
```

The remaining boundary includes nonzero Weyl curvature and Ricci-flat nonflat points. Neither is settled by this review or by Z's fixed-rank argument.
