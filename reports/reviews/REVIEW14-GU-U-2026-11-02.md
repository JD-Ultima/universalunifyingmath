## U5: why the local conclusion follows

**Jet stability.** At a fixed positive fibre matrix, a fixed augmented minor is a continuous rational function of the finite Levi-Civita connection jet, with nonzero denominators near the certified jet. Its nonvanishing persists on an open neighbourhood of admissible jets. There is no claimed radius or uniform neighbourhood over all amplitudes and metric scales.

**Persistence over nearby base points.** For each fixed metric in the family, choose one nonzero minor and keep its 43 rows and positive coordinate fibre matrix fixed. Continuity in the base point gives a neighbourhood `V` on which this same witness is nonzero in every fibre. Curvature remains nonzero there after shrinking `V`. The rational-minor argument excludes a fibre-open consistent patch over every point of `V`, even when the candidate field domain avoids the witness matrices.

This step is essential: obstruction only in the fibre over 0 would not by itself exclude a line-equation patch whose projection misses 0. U5 now states the required base-neighbourhood argument explicitly.

**Constant and variable pieces.** Within S.0's slaved vector sector, a constant-field open piece has zero even field and makes Z1 Einstein. S5 would force the base to be flat on its open projection. The nonzero curvature on `V` excludes such pieces, so `int Z` is empty. Q5(a)'s metric-free argument gives a dense nonempty `U*={dt!=0}` and `u=0`.

On a patch in `U*`, `t` ranges over an interval. The nonzero rational function `H(t)` cannot vanish throughout that interval. After shrinking, `H!=0`; `det M!=0` keeps its denominators regular. A local reparametrization with `F'!=0` gives `mu=x*F'/H!=0`, so the line equation implies the forbidden relaxed system. This uses the local algebraic/differential part of Q5, not its section-specific conclusions at type-(i) or type-(iii) points.

Thus a solution domain meeting the fibre over 0 can be restricted over `V`, where neither constant-field pieces nor a variable-field patch can survive. No analyticity or complex-cover continuation is needed for this U5 argument.
