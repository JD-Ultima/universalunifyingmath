\begin{abstract}
Wissner-Gross \cite{wg} proposes
$\alpha_{\rm cand}=(79+48\sqrt2+42\sqrt3+3\sqrt6)/31104$, with $\alpha_{\rm cand}^{-1}=137.035\,999\,189\,566\ldots$, as a parameter-free candidate for $\alpha(0)$. It is derived from an axiom about maximally entangled qudit pairs. We report five results.
(i) An independent operator-level reconstruction reproduces the $172$ records, the sector sizes, and the value exactly.
(ii) The two ``commuting exchanges'' that sort the records are a single involution on all product records. The weight law is therefore a function of exchange parity and slot class alone.
(iii) The selection of the qutrit--qubit link by an ``acting central charge'' depends on a conjugation convention for the far end of a link, which the axiom does not fix. Under the conjugate convention a second configuration satisfies every hypothesis of the uniqueness theorem. We verify this in Lean~4 against the author's own definitions.
(iv) The records as defined are not covariant under the symmetry group of their own joint state. A covariant repair leaves the value unchanged, and invariance alone leaves a nine-parameter (real) family of readouts. The weights have an exact operator form in the model's primitives, but its qutrit projector is the end rule itself; no construction local in the original frame and no short multiplicative one reproduces them, and a frame-local one after transport needs two further inputs.
(v) In a declared space of $46\,080$ rule variants that contains all twelve weight-law alternatives of \cite[Table S5]{wg}, the candidate is the unique value within $1\sigma$ of CODATA~2022. We report density reference calculations with explicit assumptions; these are not calibrated $p$-values. A second family of $332\,308$ mutants, generated mechanically from the author's Lean definitions, has the candidate as its only value within relative $10^{-4}$ of $1/\alpha$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
We follow the notation of \cite{wg}. The object is a qutrit shared by three qubit links. Two of them are read, and the qutrit is carried as a copy per link. The four \emph{slots} $Q_1,q_1,Q_2,q_2$ carry the letters: Gell-Mann matrices at $Q_i$ and Pauli matrices at $q_i$. The records are
\begin{itemize}
\item the $169$ two-slot products, recombined into eigenvectors of the slot exchange $E$ (induced by $(Q_1,q_1,Q_2,q_2)\mapsto(Q_2,q_2,Q_1,q_1)$);
\item three commutator records $[\sigma_\mu,\sigma_\nu]^{(q_1)}-[\sigma_\mu,\sigma_\nu]^{(q_2)}$.
\end{itemize}
The value is $N^{-1}\sum_r\sqrt{k_r}$ with $N=24\cdot36^2$.

\section{Reproduction}
We built the records as explicit $36\times36$ operators on $\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^2$ and checked the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item the slot-pair sizes are $(24,24,64,24,24,9)$;
\item $E$ fixes $11$ products and pairs $158$;
\item the $172$ records are Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal;
\item the sector sizes are $79/48/42/3$.
\end{itemize}
Exact summation gives the closed form. Its inverse agrees with all $51$ digits printed in \cite[Sec.~S7]{wg}, and with the $60$ digits printed by the ancillary script \texttt{enumerate\_records.py}. The author's Lean file builds under Lean~4.35.0 with axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} and \texttt{Classical.choice} only.

\section{The two exchanges coincide}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:class}
On the span of the product records, the exchange of reads $X$ and the exchange of links $Y$ of \cite{wg} act by the same involution $E$. Let $e_r\in\{\pm1\}$ be the $E$-eigenvalue of record $r$. The squared weight of \cite[Eq.~(5)]{wg} is
\[
k_r=\begin{cases}1,& e_r=-1 \text{ (product)},\\ 3,& e_r=+1,\ \text{both letters at one end},\\ 2,& e_r=+1,\ \text{one letter at each end},\\ 6,& \text{commutator record}.\end{cases}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On slots, both exchanges are the permutation $Q_1\leftrightarrow Q_2$, $q_1\leftrightarrow q_2$. They differ only in the set of records each is declared to act on: same-end records for $X$, mixed records for $Y$, and commutator records for both \cite[Sec.~S7]{wg}. Substituting into $k_r=3^{[X_r=-1]}2^{[Y_r=-1]}$ gives the table. In Lean this is \texttt{weight\_is\_class\_function}, proved by kernel evaluation on the author's definitions.
\end{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:class} isolates the content of the weight law in two assignments, $\sqrt3$ to even same-end records and $\sqrt2$ to even mixed records. In \cite{wg} these follow from wording clauses (``a read is an act at the end at which it is taken''; ``the copies of a shared qudit are one qudit''). Rewording them gives $1/\text{value}\in[112.4,146.5]$ \cite[Table S5]{wg}.

\section{Convention dependence of the selection}
The identification algebra of a $(d_1,d_2)$ link in \cite{wg} is $s(u(d_1)\oplus u(d_2))$, traceless on $\mathbb C^{d_1}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_2}$. It acts on $\mathbb C^{d_1}\otimes\mathbb C^{d_2}$ by $\xi_1\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi_2$. The pair state, however, is invariant under $U\otimes\bar U$, so the far end transforms contragrediently.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conv}
Let $c=(ix\mathbf 1_{d_1},iy\mathbf 1_{d_2})$ be central with $d_1x+d_2y=0$, $x\neq0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] If the far block acts by $\xi_2$, then $c$ acts on the link by $i(x+y)$. This is nonzero iff $d_1\neq d_2$.
\item[(A$^*$)] If the far block acts by $\bar\xi_2$, then $c$ acts by $i(x-y)=ix(d_1+d_2)/d_2\neq0$ for every type.
\item[(B)] If instead the phase constraint is imposed on the matched directions, $x+y=0$, the centre acts trivially for every type.
\end{enumerate}
Among readable types $\{(2,2),(2,3)\}$, reading A selects $(2,3)$ alone, A$^*$ selects both, and B selects neither. Under A the link has hypercharge $1/6$, the quantum numbers of $Q_L$. Under A$^*$ the link is $(\bar3,2)_{5/6}$, one of the two off-diagonal blocks of the $SU(5)$ adjoint.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct substitution of $y=-d_1x/d_2$ in (A) and (A$^*$). In (B) the action is identically zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The trace condition defining the $u(1)$ is not invariant under $\xi_2\mapsto\bar\xi_2$. Write $\eta_2=\bar\xi_2$ for the operator that actually acts at the far end under A$^*$. Since $\operatorname{tr}\eta_2=-\operatorname{tr}\xi_2$, the condition $\operatorname{tr}\xi_1+\operatorname{tr}\xi_2=0$ reads $\operatorname{tr}\xi_1-\operatorname{tr}\eta_2=0$ in terms of the acting operators. The two readings therefore define different subgroups of $U(d_1)\times U(d_2)$. Both give a four-dimensional stabilizer of the $(2,3)$ pair state, so the stabilizer does not discriminate between them.
\end{remark}

An independent enumeration reproduces the $76$ configurations of \cite[Sec.~S5]{wg}, of which $42$ are admitted by the read cap and $23$ are readable. Imposing the end rule, the two-point definition and the acting charge selects one configuration under A (the object), two under A$^*$, and none under B. Under A$^*$ the second configuration has readout $(30+30\sqrt2)/2304$, i.e.\ $1/\text{value}=31.81$. This uses the dimension-dependent form of the weight law ($\sqrt d$ per surviving exchange), which \cite[Sec.~S5, Table S5]{wg} itself uses for its $(2,2)$ entries. A further rule would be needed to choose between the two configurations.

In Lean (appended to the unmodified file of \cite{wg}) we prove \texttt{uniqueness\_fails\_under\_conjugate\_convention}. With the acting-charge predicate replaced by its A$^*$ form, the configuration ``shared qubit, two read and one unread $(2,2)$ link'' satisfies all hypotheses of the upstream uniqueness theorem and differs from the object. Moreover, the upstream Lean predicate \texttt{readable} used by \texttt{configuration\_unique} is $d_1,d_2\le3$, not the read cap $d_1d_2\le6$. Under A$^*$ the configuration ``shared qutrit, two read and one unread $(3,3)$ link'' therefore also satisfies the Lean hypotheses (\texttt{third\_config\_under\_conjugate\_convention}), although the read cap excludes it. Theorem~1 of \cite{wg} is thus correct under A. Its scope depends on a convention whose justification in \cite[Secs.~S3--S4]{wg} uses the direct sum $\mathbb C^{d_1}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_2}$, a space the axiom does not supply.

\section{Covariance of the record set}
Let $G_\Omega$ be the symmetry group of the joint read state $\lvert\omega_3\rangle_{Q_1Q_2}\otimes\lvert\omega_2\rangle_{q_1q_2}$: $U\otimes\bar U$ on $(Q_1,Q_2)$ and $V\otimes\bar V$ on $(q_1,q_2)$. The plain slot swap $E$ of \cite{wg} does not commute with $G_\Omega$, because it identifies the two read links' bases by the identity rather than by the transpose. As a result, neither the $E$-even/odd splitting nor the span of the commutator records is $G_\Omega$-invariant. Replacing $E$ with $E'=E\circ T$ (transposition at each slot) restores covariance. $E'$ is a linear anti-automorphism, $E'(AB)=E'(B)E'(A)$. On Hermitian operators it agrees with the antiunitary swap $\Theta:A\mapsto S\bar AS^{\mathsf T}$, which also commutes with $G_\Omega$. Write $D_\mu=\sigma_\mu^{(q_1)}-\sigma_\mu^{(q_2)}$ and $S_\mu=\sigma_\mu^{(q_1)}+\sigma_\mu^{(q_2)}$. Because $\sigma_y^{\mathsf T}=-\sigma_y$, the $E'$-odd commutator-type records are $D_x$, $D_z$ and $S_y$. $\Theta$ gives the same span on the Hermitian normalisation, but on the anti-Hermitian commutators of \S1 it gives a different span with the same count. With $E'$ the sector sizes, hence the value, are unchanged. Whether an exchange may act on records as an anti-automorphism is not settled by \cite{wg}, which defines the exchanges on reads and links rather than on operators. Using $E'$ in the order-sign survival rule is therefore our reconstruction choice. Two features of \cite{wg} support it: a read carries a letter to the far end by the transpose identity $(A\otimes\mathbf 1)\lvert\omega\rangle=(\mathbf 1\otimes A^{\mathsf T})\lvert\omega\rangle$ \cite[Sec.~S2]{wg}, and $A\mapsto A^{\mathsf T}$ is an anti-automorphism; and $X$ acts on a commutator record by reordering its two letters \cite[Sec.~S7]{wg}, which is what an anti-automorphism does, $E'([A,B])=[E'(B),E'(A)]$.

Under $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times\langle E'\rangle$ the covariant record space has ten isotypic components and a commutant of dimension $22$. An invariant dressing therefore has a trace with ten complex parameters. A dressing that maps Hermitian records to Hermitian records gives $10$ and $\overline{10}$ complex-conjugate weights, since $A\mapsto A^\dagger$ exchanges the two components. Only their real part enters the trace, so there are nine real parameters. The weights of \cite{wg} are one point in this family. They distinguish two copies of the irreducible $(1,3)_-$, which together form one isotypic component, giving weight $1$ to antisymmetric qubit products and $\sqrt6$ to commutator records. Symmetry does not supply that distinction.

\paragraph{Operator form of the weights.} Let $\hat N_Q,\hat N_q$ count the occupied qutrit and qubit slots of a record ($1-\tau_s$ summed over slots, with $\tau_s$ the normalised partial trace at slot $s$), and put $\sigma=(-1)^{\hat N_Q+\hat N_q}$, $S=\tfrac12(1+\sigma E')$, $\Pi_X=[\hat N_Q\hat N_q=0]$, $\Pi_Y=[\hat N_q=1]$. On the covariant record space the dressed insertion is exactly
\[ M=\bigl(1+(\sqrt3-1)S\Pi_X\bigr)\bigl(1+(\sqrt2-1)S\Pi_Y\bigr). \]
This does not derive the weights. $S\Pi_X$ gives the qutrit factor $\sqrt3$ to nine records that carry no qutrit letter (the six even qubit--qubit records and the three commutator records): it is the end-rule clause written as a projector. A rule that credits an end's dimension only to records with a letter at that end gives these records $k\in\{1,2,4,\dots\}$, so it cannot give $3$ and $6$; the alternative ``$X$ where the letters sit'' of \cite[Table S5]{wg} ($1/\text{value}=139.030$) is such a rule. In declared families (all $56\,355$ words of length $\le3$ in $38$ primitives of the model, and $321\,489$ survival--attribution dressings) no word and no such local dressing reproduces the weights. Every primitive is $\mathbb Q(i)$-rational, so no $\mathbb Q(i)$-linear combination of products of primitives, of any length, has diagonal proportional to $\sqrt{k_r}$. Transporting qubit letters to the qutrit copies through the read links reproduces the weights only if the two exchanges are assessed in different frames and the transported letters, which are supported on a rank-$2$ subspace of $\mathbb C^3$, receive the rank-$3$ join credit $\sqrt3$; the transport's own symmetry gives $2/\sqrt3$ or $\sqrt2$ ($1/\text{value}=140.702$ or $139.030$).

\section{Declared rule-space sensitivity}
Table S5 of \cite{wg} lists the computed value and fifteen closed-form alternatives. The computed row and the twelve weight-law alternatives are reproduced from the record classes. The other three are read-end counting ($70.19$), normalisation by the $172$ records ($0.758$), and the smallest readable configuration ($3.535$). They change the counting unit, the normalisation or the configuration, and all lie far from $137$. We then declared a rule space that contains the twelve:
\begin{itemize}
\item an independent squared factor $k\in\{1,2,3,6\}$ for each of the four product classes;
\item a commutator factor in $\{1,2,3,4,6,9\}$ and count in $\{0,3,6\}$;
\item amplitude or Born weighting;
\item five normalizations $N\in\{24\cdot36^2,36^3,24^3,24\cdot35^2,35^3\}$.
\end{itemize}
This gives $46\,080$ rules and $15\,639$ distinct values. The rule of \cite{wg} is the closest to CODATA~2022, at $0.598\sigma$. The next closest is at $1.3\times10^5\sigma$. In $(120,155)$ the distinct values have density $103.9$ per unit. For an illustrative random-target model, let $U$ be uniform on $(120,155)$ while the outputs $v_j$ stay fixed. The reference expected match count is
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\#\{j:|v_j-U|\le\sigma\}\right]
 =\frac1{35}\sum_j\bigl|[v_j-\sigma,v_j+\sigma]\cap(120,155)\bigr|
 =4.4\times10^{-6},
\]
where $\sigma=2.1\times10^{-8}$. This expectation belongs to the stated artificial target model. It is not a calibrated $p$-value for the measured value of $\alpha$.

Separately, among the $1.63\times10^9$ elements $(a+b\sqrt2+c\sqrt3+d\sqrt6)/31104$ with $0\le a,b,c,d\le200$, exactly one lies within $1\sigma$: $(79,48,42,3)$. Multiplying the number of admissible $(b,c,d)$ triples by the numerator-window width gives the uniform-fractional-part reference count $0.024$. The actual fractional parts are deterministic, so this is a density heuristic rather than a calibrated expectation for the construction.

As a Lean-side check we ran the author's mutation harness, which applies $19$ single-definition rewordings and has Lean~4.35.0 evaluate each readout. Of the $19$, thirteen lie in the declared space, and Lean reproduces our numerator exactly for each. Ten of these are Table S5 rows reproduced above. Three more are in the declared space only: $205.55$ (normalisation $36^3$), $150.79$ and $138.94$. Four are outside it, at $133.97$, $254.54$, $149.59$ and $295.10$. Two leave the value unchanged by construction. Apart from those two, none is within $10^{-3}$ of $\alpha^{-1}$.

The declared space is our choice. As a second declared family we generated mutants mechanically from the Lean definitions. Fifteen standard mutation operators (literal changes and swaps, negation, relational, logical, quantifier and arithmetic operator replacement, branch and match-arm swaps, swaps of declared end and role labels, list entries dropped or duplicated) were applied at all $425$ sites of the $44$ definitions on which the readout depends. The family consists of the $818$ single mutants and the $331\,490$ pairs with disjoint sites. Each mutant is evaluated exactly by a Python transcription of the definitions, which agrees with Lean~4.35.0 on all $818$ singles and on a seeded sample of $300$ pairs. The $272\,988$ well-defined mutants take $10\,340$ distinct values, with density $31.6$ per unit near $1/\alpha$ (the declared space: $103.9$). The only mutant value within relative $10^{-4}$ of $1/\alpha$ is the candidate's own; the next closest is $9.9\times10^5\sigma$ away. The two families share only $333$ distinct values. The density grows with the number of combined edits (about $1.1$ per unit for single mutants, $31.6$ with pairs), so it describes this family at depth two, not a bound.

These computations establish how the value changes within declared bounded families. A calibrated probability of agreement would additionally require a justified null distribution for constructions or targets and a specified selection procedure. A finite rule count, or fixing one rule before evaluation, does not supply that distribution. The choice of family and of a uniform-target band also affects the reference count. We therefore infer no single-rule probability or threshold number of additional rules from it. Reference~\cite{wg} does not document the order of rule choices and evaluation; that omission establishes neither a search nor its size, and we infer no author intent. Further measurements test the fixed numerical conjecture directly.

\section{Testability}
The candidate deviates from the measurements as follows: $+0.60\sigma$ (CODATA 2022), $+1.57\sigma$ (electron $g-2$), $-1.49\sigma$ (Rb), $+5.32\sigma$ (Cs). These figures use CODATA 2022 \cite{codata}, the electron $g-2$ \cite{fan}, Rb recoil \cite{morel} and Cs recoil \cite{parker}. Consistent determinations with absolute uncertainty $u\lesssim2.5\times10^{-9}$ in $\alpha^{-1}$ (relative $1.8\times10^{-11}$) would test it at $5\sigma$, if centred near the present CODATA value. The identification also requires a map from the hypercharge readout to $\alpha_{\rm em}=\alpha_Y\cos^2\theta_W$, which \cite{wg} defers.
