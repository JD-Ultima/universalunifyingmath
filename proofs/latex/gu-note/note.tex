\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct, as precise mathematics, the finite-dimensional and first-order parts of E.~Weinstein's \emph{Geometric Unity: Author's Working Draft v1.0} (1 April 2021) \cite{W21}, and test them.

\emph{Signature.} The natural fiber metrics on $Y=\Met(X^4)$ form the DeWitt family $G_\lambda$. Among bases of signature $(1,3)$, $(3,1)$ and $(4,0)$, the chimeric bundle has signature $(7,7)$ if and only if the base is Lorentzian, $\lambda>\tfrac14$, and a fixed relative block sign holds. A base of signature $(2,2)$ also gives $(7,7)$, for every $\lambda>\tfrac14$ and either block sign. Over $\Met(X)$ itself the chimeric bundle admits no $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ structure, for any $X$, both for a Lorentzian base and for a base of signature $(2,2)$. For a Lorentzian base, over the time-oriented double cover that the draft writes in (3.33), it admits one if and only if $w_2(X)=0$.

\emph{Spinors.} One Majorana--Weyl spinor of $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$, restricted to $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(6,4)$, is exactly one Standard Model generation with $\nu_R$. The draft's three tables of quantum numbers are reproduced exactly. In Standard Model quantum numbers, the Rarita--Schwinger $144$ carries a further full generation.

\emph{Gauge equivariance.} Two equivariance lemmas of the inhomogeneous gauge group are false as printed. One global sign convention restores consistency, but it changes at least two printed formulas.

\emph{Field equations.} For the natural reconstructed family of ``Shiab'' operators, the printed field equation is not the Euler--Lagrange equation of the printed action. Under stated hypotheses, the same holds for every nonzero admissible Shiab operator.

\emph{Reduction.} Along a section, a natural observerse metric reproduces the 4D Einstein equation with a cosmological term in the projected sense: for the pulled-back Einstein tensor of $Y$, and for the reduced action. At zero displacement the 14-dimensional equations do not hold on the section. Under one of the two readings of the draft (Z2) the cosmological term has the anti-de Sitter sign. The term depends on choices the draft does not make. With the displacement field included and a self-adjoint Shiab operator with nonzero Einstein output, the field equation has no solution with vanishing displacement near the section; in the invariant sector (where the even part of the displacement vanishes) in signature $(7,7)$, for every operator $xW_A+yW'_A+zW_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma+wW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ with $(x,y)\neq0$, it has no solution near the section at all. Once the even part is nonzero, the elliptic twist enters the reduced equations. With the even part slaved to an odd part of the invariant form, and an isotropy-invariant 2-jet along the section, the field equation at the points of the section has solutions in both signatures $(14,0)$ and $(7,7)$, for instance for $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$, with the cosmological term not fixed.

\emph{Neighbourhoods of the section.} Under Z2, for $\kappa\neq0$ and the slaved even part with invertible slaving block ($\det M\neq0$), and whatever the coefficients of the odd part, the field equation has no solution on any open set meeting the section, in $(14,0)$ or $(7,7)$, for every member with $(x,y)\neq0$. It has none on any open set at all, except possibly, up to symmetry, for $x(W_A+cW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma)$ with $c\neq0$ ($c^2\neq\tfrac{11}6$ in $(14,0)$) on open sets away from the section. For $(x,y)=0$ the only solution in this sector is $T=0$, for every metric. Under Z1, with the same hypotheses, for $W_A$, $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ and every member with $xz\pm yw\neq0$ and $x^2\pm y^2\neq0$ (upper sign $(14,0)$), the field equation has no solution near the section in $(7,7)$. In $(14,0)$ a solution near the section requires $\lambda=\tfrac15$ and a Ricci-flat base, and for real-analytic fields it has a flat base, a constant odd part and a vanishing even part. For $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ ($c_V>0$) such solutions exist if and only if $c_V\kappa^2\ge9360$, and they solve the field equation on all of $Y$, with vanishing 4D cosmological term. The Z1 argument is partly computer-assisted.

\emph{Variation of the base metric.} The draft also varies the base metric (its $\gimel$). A compactly supported variation of it is not compactly supported on $Y$, so this equation needs a weight on the noncompact fiber. In every case giving $(7,7)$ no nonzero natural weight function has a finite fiber integral, and only natural weights carried by the multiples of the base metric (the scaling ray through the section, and its negative for a base of signature $(2,2)$) make the action of the invariant sector converge for every base. For these, in the invariant sector with constant odd part and vanishing even part, the $\gimel$-equation alone, off-shell at fixed displacement, is exactly the 4D vacuum Einstein equation whenever its Einstein term is nonzero. As the displacement tends to zero, its cosmological term tends to the reduced-action value $\Lambda_A$ under Z2 and to $r_\mu\Lambda_A$ under Z1, where $r_\mu$ is a ratio of moments of the weight. This holds with the equation on $Y$ unweighted, as the draft writes it; with the weight there too, the Einstein term drops out. In this sector $\Upsilon=0$ itself holds near the section only under Z1 in $(14,0)$, over a flat base, with vanishing 4D cosmological term.

All computations are exact or have stated tolerances, and the code and outputs are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source, method and notation}
The source is the 69-page draft \cite{W21}, obtained from a third-party mirror (sha256 \texttt{3f28d742\ldots86d4}; PDF timestamps 2021-04-01). It has not been verified against the author's host. Every statement of the draft that we use is cited by equation, section or page; page numbers are PDF pages, which equal the printed ones. Where the draft is ambiguous, we fix an explicit reconstruction, labelled R-$\ast$, and test the alternatives. Critiques of the draft were treated as leads only; none is used as evidence. Code and outputs are in \texttt{computation/geometric-unity/}, the Lean files in \texttt{proofs/lean/geometric-unity/}, and the proofs in \texttt{formalization/geometric-unity/} (write-ups A--O and J5d). The results of \S7 beyond the radial ansatz (write-ups L--O) are computed by \texttt{L\_nonradial.py}, \texttt{M\_remaining.py}, \texttt{N\_z1\_neighbourhood.py} and \texttt{O\_gimel.py}, and cross-checked by the internal referee scripts \texttt{xcheck\_round8.py}--\texttt{xcheck\_round11.py}, which import none of that code.

\emph{Notation.} $\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_{13}$ generate $\mathrm{Cl}(7,7)\cong\mathbb R(128)$, $\Gamma=\gamma_0\cdots\gamma_{13}$, and $\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma X=\Gamma X\Gamma^{-1}$ is the grade involution. The gauge algebra is $\mathfrak u(64,64)\subset M_{128}(\mathbb C)$, and the pairing is $\langle T,X\rangle=\int\mathrm{Tr}(T\wedge X)$. For algebra-valued forms $a=\sum e^I\otimes a_I$ and $b$, set $[a\wedge b]_C=\sum e^I\wedge e^J\otimes(a_Ib_J-b_Ja_I)$ and $[a\wedge b]_A=i\sum e^I\wedge e^J\otimes(a_Ib_J+b_Ja_I)$. The soldering invariants are $\Phi^1=\sum_m e^m\otimes\gamma_m$, $\Phi^2=\sum_{m<n}e^{mn}\otimes\gamma_m\gamma_n$ and $\Phi^3=\sum_{a<b<c}e^{abc}\otimes i\gamma_a\gamma_b\gamma_c$, and $\Phi_{11}={*}\Phi^3$. They are tilted as $\Phi\mapsto\varepsilon^{-1}\Phi\varepsilon$. Set $W_s\xi=[\Phi_{11}\wedge\xi]_s$ and $W'_s\xi=[\Phi_{11}\Gamma\wedge\xi]_s$ for $s\in\{A,C\}$. The reconstruction \emph{R-S} is the family
\[ \circledast^{(s_1;s_2,s_3)}_c\xi=[\Phi^1\wedge{*}\xi]_{s_1}-\tfrac c2\,{*}\bigl[\Phi^1\wedge{*}[\Phi^2\wedge{*}\xi]_{s_3}\bigr]_{s_2},\qquad s_i\in\{C,A\},\ c\in\mathbb R, \]
which reads the draft's (8.1) and (9.2)--(9.3). For a group element $g=(\varepsilon,\varpi)$ (\S4), $A=A_0+\varpi$ and $B=A_0\cdot\varepsilon$ are the two connections, and $T=\varpi-\varepsilon^{-1}d_{A_0}\varepsilon$ is the augmented torsion \cite[(7.3)]{W21}. The projection of \cite[(6.11)]{W21} is $\pi(g)=\varepsilon\varpi\varepsilon^{-1}+(d_{A_0}\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-1}$, and $\pi'(g)=\varepsilon\varpi\varepsilon^{-1}-(d_{A_0}\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-1}$. $\kappa=\kappa_1$ is the coefficient of the torsion term in (9.4) and (9.7). $\Upsilon$ and $\Xi$ are the $\varpi$- and $\varepsilon$-gradients of the action (9.4), as in (9.5)--(9.6). $\Upsilon^{\rm pr}=\circledast F_A+\kappa{*}T$ is the expression printed in (9.7). $\delta_1$ (the infinitesimal gauge action) and $\delta_2$ (the linearisation of $\Upsilon$) are the maps of the deformation sequence (10.1).

\section{Fiber metrics and the chimeric signature}
Let $Y=\Met_{(p,q)}(X^n)$. Its vertical bundle is $V=\pi^*\mathrm{Sym}^2T^*X$. A $GL(n)$-natural fiber metric is, up to scale, one of the DeWitt family \cite{DW}
\[ G_\lambda(h,k)=\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}hg^{-1}k)-\lambda\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}h)\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}k). \]
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sig}
Set $P=\binom{p+1}2+\binom{q+1}2$. Then $\operatorname{sig}G_\lambda=(P,pq)$ for $\lambda<1/n$ and $(P-1,pq+1)$ for $\lambda>1/n$. Let $n=4$, $\lambda\neq\tfrac14$ and $g_C=c_VG_\lambda\oplus c_Hg^{-1}$ on $C=V\oplus\pi^*T^*X$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a base of signature $(1,3)$, $(3,1)$ or $(4,0)$, $\operatorname{sig}g_C=(7,7)$ if and only if the base is Lorentzian, $\lambda>\tfrac14$, and $c_Hg^{-1}$ has exactly one eigenvalue of the sign of $c_V$. A Riemannian base never gives $(7,7)$. A base of signature $(2,2)$ gives $(7,7)$ for every $\lambda>\tfrac14$ and either block sign.
\item For $n\ge2$ and $\lambda\neq1/n$, the chimeric signature is neutral exactly for bases $(1,3)$, $(3,1)$, $(2,2)$, giving $(7,7)$, and $(2,3)$, $(3,2)$, giving $(10,10)$; in every case $\lambda>1/n$. For $n=1$, the signature $(1,1)$ occurs on both sides of $\lambda=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sig} is checked in \texttt{fiber\_signature.out} and \texttt{xcheck\_dewitt.out} (write-up A). The $(7,7)$ choice of \cite[\S3.5, p.~22]{W21} is therefore available, but not forced. It needs the DeWitt parameter on the supermetric side and a relative sign.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:spin}
In the Lorentzian $(7,7)$ case of Theorem~\ref{thm:sig}, let $\ell\subset\pi^*TX$ be the time line of the tautological metric. Then $w_1(C)=0$ and $w_2(C)=\pi^*w_2(X)$. Moreover $C=C_+\oplus C_-$ with $w_1(C_+)=w_1(C_-)=w_1(\ell)$. The $SO(7,7)$ frame bundle of $C$ lifts to $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ if and only if
\[ w_2(C_+)+w_2(C_-)=\pi^*w_2(X)+w_1(\ell)^2=0 . \]
Hence over $Y=\Met(X)$ no $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ structure on $C$ exists, for any $X$. Over the time-oriented double cover $Y'$ of \cite[(3.33), p.~24]{W21} one exists if and only if $w_2(X)=0$, that is, if and only if $X$ is spin when $X$ is orientable.

For a base of signature $(2,2)$, let $P,N\subset\pi^*TX$ be the positive and negative planes of the tautological metric. Then $w_1(C_+)=w_1(C_-)=w_1(N)$ and the obstruction is $\pi^*w_2(X)+w_1(N)^2$ (with $P$ in place of $N$ for the other block sign). This is nonzero on every fiber, so over $\Met_{(2,2)}(X)$ no $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ structure on $C$ exists either, for any $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The criterion $w_2(E_+)+w_2(E_-)=0$ for an indefinite bundle is due to Karoubi \cite{K68}; see also \cite{B81}. It differs from $w_2(E)$ by $w_1(E_+)w_1(E_-)$. To verify it, reduce to the maximal compact subgroup $S(O(7)\times O(7))$, whose classifying space has $H^2(\,\cdot\,;\mathbb Z_2)$ spanned by $w_2(C_+)$, $w_2(C_-)$ and $w_1(C_\pm)^2$. A reflection in two orthonormal vectors lifts to $\pm\gamma_a\gamma_b$. Here $(\gamma_a\gamma_b)^2=-1$ when the two vectors have the same sign and $+1$ when their signs differ, which fixes the extension class.

For $c_V,c_H>0$ and $\lambda>\tfrac14$, put $\Sigma=\ell^\perp$. Then $C_+\simeq\mathrm{Sym}^2_0\Sigma^*\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\ell^*$ and $C_-\simeq\mathbb Rg\oplus(\ell^*\otimes\Sigma^*)\oplus\Sigma^*$, and the splitting principle gives the classes. The fiber of $\Met(X)$ is homotopy equivalent to $\mathbb{RP}^3$, and $\ell$ restricts to the tautological line there, so $w_1(\ell)^2\neq0$ on every fiber. On $Y'$ the line $\ell$ is oriented, and $\pi^*$ is injective on $H^2$ by the Gysin sequence. Every step is checked exactly in \texttt{A\_spin\_obstruction.out}.

For the base $(2,2)$, $c_V,c_H>0$ and $\lambda>\tfrac14$: $C_+\simeq\mathrm{Sym}^2_0P^*\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^2_0N^*\oplus\mathbb R\oplus P^*$ and $C_-\simeq\mathbb Rg\oplus(P^*\otimes N^*)\oplus N^*$; for $c_H<0$ exchange $P^*$ and $N^*$ in the last summands, and an overall sign change swaps $C_\pm$. The splitting principle gives the classes. On a fiber $P\oplus N$ is trivial, so $w_1(P)=w_1(N)$ there, and $u\mapsto(2uu^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf 1_3)\oplus1$, $u\in S^2$, embeds $\mathbb{RP}^2$ in the fiber with $N=u^\perp\subset\mathbb R^3$, the complement of the tautological line $\gamma$, so $w_1(N)^2=w_1(\gamma)^2\neq0$. These steps are checked exactly in \texttt{A\_spin\_obstruction\_22.out}.
\end{proof}
The draft therefore must, not merely may, use the space $Y'$ of (3.33) rather than $\Met(X)$ of Def.~3.1 for the bundle $P_{\widetilde{\mathrm{Fr}}(C)}$ of (3.32).

\section{Spinor content}
\begin{theorem}
$\mathrm{Cl}(7,7)\cong\mathbb R(128)$, and Majorana--Weyl spinors exist. A Majorana--Weyl spinor restricted to $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(6,4)$ is $((2,1)\otimes16)_{\mathbb R}$. Under $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, embedded via the Pati--Salam maximal compact subgroup $\mathrm{Spin}(6)\times\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ \cite{BH}, this is one generation with $\nu^c$.

Take the field content $\nu\in S^+$, $\zeta\in T^*Y\otimes S^-$ of \cite[(12.28)]{W21}. In the Majorana--Weyl reading, its left-handed spin-$\tfrac12$ part is $3\times16_+\oplus144_-$. In the complex Weyl reading it is $6\times16_+\oplus2\times144_-$, and in the Dirac reading it is vector-like. The module $144_-=\ker(V\otimes S^-\to S^+)$ contains the Standard Model content of one further generation, together with 128 exotic states, including states of charge $\pm\tfrac43$, $\pm\tfrac53$ and $\pm2$.
\end{theorem}
The draft's three tables of quantum numbers \cite[pp.~52--53]{W21} are reproduced exactly. The $SU(3)$ conjugation bars of the p.~53 table were transcribed from a 220-dpi render of that page. The $144_-$ content was verified by two independent methods: weight multisets, and an explicit $\mathrm{Cl}(10)$ realisation with Casimir and triality labels (\texttt{spinor\_branching.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_144.out}). The draft's ``$2+1$'' (two generations plus an ``imposter'', \cite[(12.22), p.~62]{W21}) counts $\mathrm{Spin}(6,4)$-$16_+$ multiplets. At the level of Standard Model quantum numbers the count is four. With complex Dirac spinors the content is vector-like, and ``$\mathrm{Spin}(14,\mathbb C)\to U(128)$'' \cite[(3.20)]{W21} is ill-typed. Theorem~\ref{thm:full}(4) shows that the Majorana--Weyl reading leaves $\zeta$ without a kinetic term. The count $3\times16_+\oplus144_-$ therefore belongs to a reading that the dynamics excludes.

\section{The inhomogeneous gauge group}
Write $\mathcal G=\mathcal H\ltimes\Omega^1(\mathrm{ad})$ with $(\varepsilon_1,\varpi_1)(\varepsilon_2,\varpi_2)=(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_2^{-1}\varpi_1\varepsilon_2+\varpi_2)$, and let $c(h)=h^{-1}d_{A_0}h$.
\begin{proposition}
$\tau(h)=(h,-c(h))$ is an injective homomorphism onto the right stabiliser of $A_0$, and $\pi$ is $\tau$-invariant (Lemma~6.3 of \cite{W21}). Lemmas 6.5 and 7.2 of \cite{W21} are false as printed:
\[ \mu(g\tau(h))-\mu(g)\cdot h=(-2c(h),0),\qquad T_{g\tau(h)}-h^{-1}T_gh=-2c(h). \]
They hold for $\tau'(h)=(h,+c(h))$, the left stabiliser, but then Lemma~6.3 fails:
\[ \pi(g\tau'(h))-\pi(g)=2\,\varepsilon\,(d_{A_0}h)h^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}. \]
The map $(\varepsilon,\varpi)\mapsto(\varepsilon,-\varpi)$ is an automorphism of $\mathcal G$ exchanging $\tau$ and $\tau'$. Sections 6--7 of \cite{W21} become consistent after one global sign choice, but no single printed formula can be changed alone. Either
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] replace $\tau$ by $\tau'$ in (6.4) and in its expansion (6.13), and $\pi$ by $\pi'$ in (6.11); if the left action of Lemma~6.4 is to remain the one induced through the projection, also flip the sign of $(d_{A_0}\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-1}$ in (6.15). This agrees with (6.18), (6.19), (6.22), (7.3) and (10.6) as printed; or
\item[(B)] keep $\tau$, $\pi$ and (6.15), and flip the sign of $\varpi$ in $\mu$ (6.18)--(6.19), in the connection $A_\omega=\nabla_0+\varpi_\omega$ of (6.22), and in $T$ (7.3). This conflicts with (10.6), which is computed with the sign of $\tau'$: under (B) its right-hand side becomes $(\varepsilon\gamma,-d_{A_0-\varpi}\gamma)$, for $\gamma$ in the Lie algebra of $\mathcal H$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
All identities are exact (\texttt{gauge\_group\_identities.out}, \texttt{C\_tilt\_repair.out} R1--R7); the group-level statements, including the failure of the printed Lemmas 6.5 and 7.2, are also proved in Lean in \texttt{GUTilt.lean}. Not counting proof lines, (A) changes three displayed formulas, or four with (6.15). (B) changes four, or five if (6.18) and (6.19) are counted separately. The draft warns of ``2 or more conflicting conventions in this section'' \cite[fn.~7, p.~36]{W21}. We use (A), which agrees with the draft's own (10.6), and call it R-C1$'$.

\section{Shiab operators and the field equation}
For R-S, the soldering invariants $\Phi^1,\Phi^2$ define the family $\circledast^{(s_1;s_2,s_3)}_c:\Omega^2(\mathrm{ad})\to\Omega^{13}(\mathrm{ad})$ of \S1.
\begin{proposition}
The member $(C;A,A)$ at $c=1$ maps the Levi-Civita spin curvature to $-{*}G$, with $G$ the Einstein tensor of $Y^{14}$. Every member annihilates the Weyl part. For every member of R-S, that is for all eight bracket choices and every $c$, $\circledast\circ d_B$ fails to be formally self-adjoint: there are $T=Xe^0$ and $\alpha=Ye^1$ with $X,Y$ Clifford monomials (times $i$ where needed), for which the pointwise forms of the self-adjointness criterion satisfy $\beta_0(T,\alpha)=\pm256$ for every $c$ and $\beta_0(\alpha,T)=0$. Hence the printed equation (9.7) is not the Euler--Lagrange equation of the printed action (9.4) within R-S.
\end{proposition}
The certificates for all eight choices are in \texttt{C\_rs\_all\_members.out}. A random-sample test gives relative defects of at least $0.84$ at the least-squares optimal $c$. Over the whole equivariant space $\mathcal S=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)}(\Lambda^2\otimes\mathfrak u(64,64),\Lambda^{13}\otimes\mathfrak u(64,64))$ the answer is sharper:
\begin{theorem}
$\dim\mathcal S=200$. The subspace on which $\circledast\circ d_B$ is formally self-adjoint has dimension $48$. It is exactly the space of operators of the form $\Psi\wedge$ with $\Psi\in\Lambda^3\otimes S^2\mathfrak g^*$. Every $\circledast\in\mathcal S$ annihilates the Weyl part of a Levi-Civita curvature, while self-adjointness forces the exact Einstein ratio. The operator $W_A=[\Phi_{11}\wedge\cdot]_A$, of the draft's form (8.1) with $r=11$, is self-adjoint and yields $-{*}G$.

Now vary $\varpi$ at fixed $\varepsilon$. Let $\circledast$ be any pointwise bundle map that does not depend on $\varpi$, and pair with a constant $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant form $b$. Then $\Upsilon^{\rm pr}$ of (9.7) is a $\varpi$-gradient only if $b(\alpha\wedge\circledast\xi)=\sum_k\Omega_k\wedge I_k(\alpha\wedge\xi)$. Here the $\Omega_k$ are 11-forms and the $I_k$ are the invariant symmetric forms of the gauge algebra. For $\mathfrak u(64,64)$ and $b=\mathrm{Tr}$ this reads $\circledast\xi=\Omega_0\wedge\xi+\Omega_1\wedge\mathrm{Tr}(\xi)\mathbf 1$, a generalised Chern--Simons operator. Such an operator preserves Clifford grade, so it never outputs the Einstein tensor. It vanishes under $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ or $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(6,4)$ symmetry. Hence, under these hypotheses, (9.7) is not variational for any nonzero admissible Shiab operator. The true Euler--Lagrange equation of (9.4) with $W_A$ is $W_AF_A+\kappa{*}T-\tfrac13[T\wedge[T\wedge\Phi_{11}]_C]_A=0$.
\end{theorem}
The dimension count and the self-adjoint subspace are checked in \texttt{D\_rep\_counts.out}, \texttt{D\_selfadjoint\_shiab.out} and \texttt{xcheck\_shiab\_dim.out}. Field-dependent multipliers and $\varpi$-dependent Shiab operators are outside the hypotheses.

\section{The complete variational system, fermions, and ellipticity}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:full}
Under the repaired tilt $\tau'$ (route (A) of \S4) the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The action factors through the projection $\pi'$ (a Stueckelberg form). Hence the $\varepsilon$-variation adds nothing, and the complete Euler--Lagrange system in $(\varepsilon,\varpi)$ is $\Upsilon=0$, with the off-shell Noether identity $\Xi\equiv d_A\Upsilon$ for the true $\Upsilon$. This identity fails for $\Upsilon^{\rm pr}$.
\item Requiring $d_\nabla(\circledast F_\nabla)=0$ for every metric selects the exact Einstein ratio.
\item Every equivariant Shiab operator on spinor-valued forms flips chirality, and the natural Clifford contraction is the Rarita--Schwinger operator. The chirality labels of (9.16) are inconsistent, and swapping one pair of columns repairs them.
\item In signature $(7,7)$ the Spin-invariant bilinears make both the Dirac and the Rarita--Schwinger kinetic terms of a single Majorana--Weyl field total derivatives. The minimal consistent real content is therefore two Majorana--Weyl copies, equivalent to complex Weyl, and the left-handed content doubles to $6\times16\oplus2\times144$.
\item $\delta_2\circ\delta_1=\mathrm{ad}(\Upsilon)$, so the deformation sequence is a complex only on solutions. Its symbol is exact at no covector, for the draft's whole self-adjoint family $\mathrm{span}(W_A,W'_A)$ and for R-S, in both $(14,0)$ and $(7,7)$. In $(7,7)$ a null-cone obstruction excludes every self-adjoint Shiab operator. The elliptic deformation complex in Euclidean signature claimed in \cite[\S12, item (x), p.~65]{W21} therefore fails for these operators. It does hold for a generic self-adjoint Shiab operator: in $(14,0)$ with $\mathfrak u(128)$ the complex is elliptic if and only if $\kappa\neq0$ and an explicit degree-$34$ polynomial $\Delta(\Psi)$ does not vanish. The self-adjoint part of the draft's form (8.1) lies inside $\{\Delta=0\}$. The operator $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ is an explicit elliptic example, and it agrees with $W_A$ on curvature.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:full} is checked in \texttt{G\_el\_noether.out}, \texttt{G\_fermion.out}, \texttt{G\_symbols.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_mw\_kinetic.out} and \texttt{H\_ellipticity.out}; the algebraic core of item~1 is proved in Lean in \texttt{GStueckelberg.lean}.

\section{The 14$\to$4 reduction}
No $\mathrm{Diff}(X)$-natural connection exists on $\Met(X)$. We therefore use the Levi-Civita connection $\aleph$ of the base metric $g$. The draft supports two horizontal blocks. In Z1 the horizontal metric is the point $y$ itself \cite[(3.7), (3.15), p.~36, p.~56]{W21}. In Z2 it is the pulled-back base metric \cite[\S3.5, p.~22]{W21}. In both readings the observerse metric is $g_\aleph=c_H\,h_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu+c_V\,G_\lambda|_y(Dy,Dy)$, with $h=y$ in Z1 and $h=g$ in Z2, where $Dy$ is the vertical part of $dy$ defined by $\aleph$. The fiber is the symmetric space $\mathbb R\times SL(n)/SO(p,q)$, whose curvature is independent of $\lambda$. The draft's 4D claim is (9.10) \cite[p.~45]{W21}.
\begin{theorem}
Along a section $s=g$, $s^*g_\aleph=c_Hg$, and the normal bundle is the vertical bundle.
\begin{itemize}
\item Under Z2, the restricted Einstein tensor of $Y$ yields $G(g)+\Lambda g$ with $\Lambda=n(n-1)(n+2)c_H/(16c_V)$ for every $\lambda$, which is $9c_H/(2c_V)$ for $n=4$ (O'Neill's formulas \cite{ON}). With the $(7,7)$ sign rule this is the anti-de Sitter sign.
\item Under Z1, $\Lambda$ depends on both $\lambda$ and the route (projection vs reduced action).
\item For $n\ge2$, $g_\aleph$ is Einstein along the section if and only if the reading is Z1, $\lambda=1/(n+1)$ and $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$. For $n=4$, that value $\lambda=\tfrac15$ forces a signature other than $(7,7)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
The theorem is checked in \texttt{F\_zorro\_reduction.out} and \texttt{F\_zorro\_einstein.out}; under Z1 it uses the Ricci blocks along the section, which write-up K proves for every $n$ (\texttt{K\_z1\_ricci.out}). The first two items are statements about the projected tensor $s^*G(g_\aleph)$ and the reduced action. The 14-dimensional Einstein tensor $G(g_\aleph)$ is nonzero on $s(X)$ for every $g$, $\lambda$, $c_V$, $c_H$, and so is the draft's $\Upsilon$ there at $T=0$. Matching the observed cosmological constant under Z2 requires a fiber length scale of about $2\times10^{26}$\,m, and the sign of the Z2 term is opposite to that of the observed value.

\paragraph{With the displacement field.} Let $\circledast$ be self-adjoint with $\kappa\neq0$ and nonzero Einstein output, and split $T=T_e+T_o$ by Clifford parity. Then the even part of $\Upsilon=0$ determines $T_e$ algebraically from $T_o$ wherever the linear map $L_{T_o}=\kappa{*}+2\mathcal B(\cdot,T_o)$ is invertible, where $\mathcal B$ is the symmetric bilinear map for which $\mathcal B(T,T)$ is the part of $\Upsilon$ quadratic in $T$. Moreover $T_o$ cannot vanish on any open set that meets the fiber-scaling cone $\{(x,e^\sigma g(x)):x\in X,\ \sigma\in\mathbb R\}$ through $s(X)$ (for real-analytic $g$, on any open set). So $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution with $T=0$ near the section. In the $\mathrm{Spin}$-invariant sector $T=t\Phi^1+u\Phi^1\Gamma$ (so $T_e\equiv0$), a solution of $\Upsilon=0$ near a point of $s(X)$ makes $g_\aleph$ Einstein there. In $(7,7)$ this has no solution for any operator $xW_A+yW'_A+zW_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma+wW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ with $(x,y)\neq0$, which includes every member that is elliptic in $(14,0)$ ($xz+yw\neq0$). In $(14,0)$ it requires Z1, $\lambda=\tfrac15$ and $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$, and then the 4D cosmological term vanishes; for $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$, with base $(4,0)$ and $c_V>0$, it also requires $c_V\kappa^2\ge9360$. The projected equation alone is underdetermined: for $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$, besides $T=0$ it admits invariant $T\neq0$ with any value of the cosmological term in $(7,7)$. The twisted part $W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ never acts on curvature and preserves these 4D algebraic equations and cosmological ranges. Its projected constraint nevertheless excludes normal derivatives of $u$ that $W_A$ permits: a 4D Bianchi identity controls only derivatives along the section. Equality of the invariant-sector solution sets on an open set of $Y$ uses the 14D identity instead. The draft gives no rule that fixes $T$ off the section (\texttt{I\_elliptic\_reduction.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round5.out}, \texttt{projection\_regression.out}).

\emph{The slaved even part.} Now let $T_o=t\Phi^1+u\Phi^1\Gamma$ with nonconstant $t,u$, and let $T_e$ range over the four copies of the vector representation in $\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak g_{\rm ev}$. On this sector the even equation closes exactly: it is a $4\times4$ linear system $M\vec A=-S\,(dt,du)^{\mathsf T}$, where $\vec A$ collects the four covector coefficients of $T_e$, $M$ is the matrix of $L_{T_o}$ on this sector, and $S$ is the $4\times2$ matrix of the source $\circledast(d_0T_o)$. So $T_e$ is explicit where $\det M\neq0$. The odd equation then contains second derivatives of $t,u$ with coefficient matrix $H=-\ell_EM^{-1}S$, where $\ell_E$ is the $2\times4$ matrix through which the first derivatives of $T_e$ enter the grade-1 and grade-13 parts of the odd equation, and $\det H=576^2(xz\pm yw)^2\kappa^2/\det M$ (upper sign $(14,0)$, lower $(7,7)$). It also contains constraints proportional to $dt\wedge du$. So the twisted coefficients $z,w$ do enter, although not the zeroth-order term. With an isotropy-invariant 2-jet of $(t,u)$ along $s(X)$, the field equation $\Upsilon=0$ at the points of $s(X)$ has solutions in both $(7,7)$ and $(14,0)$, with the cosmological term not fixed, for every member with $(x,y)\neq0$ and $(x,y)^{\mathsf T}\in\operatorname{im}H$, including $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$. So at the points of $s(X)$ the $(7,7)$ statement above is a property of $T_e\equiv0$; on open sets meeting $s(X)$ it persists for the slaved $T_e$, under Z2 for every member with $(x,y)\neq0$ and under Z1 for the members treated below. For $t,u$ functions of the fiber-scaling coordinate $\tfrac14\log\lvert\det(g(x)^{-1}y)\rvert$ alone, in Z2, in both signatures $(14,0)$ and $(7,7)$, $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution on any neighbourhood of a point of $s(X)$ for any operator with $(x,y)\neq0$, when $T_e$ is the slaved field ($\kappa\neq0$, $\det M\neq0$) (\texttt{J\_slaved\_sector.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round6.out}, \texttt{J5d\_leaf.out}).

\emph{Beyond the radial ansatz.} Keep Z2, $\kappa\neq0$ and the slaved $T_e$ with $\det M\neq0$, let $t,u$ be arbitrary, and let $\Upsilon=0$ hold on a connected open set $U\subset Y$. Write $E^{(1)},E^{(13)}$ for the grade-1 and grade-13 parts of the odd equation (symmetric 2-tensors on $Y$), $\ell=xz\pm yw$ and $N=x^2\pm y^2$ (upper sign $(14,0)$), and $E=y\,\partial_y$ for the Euler field of the fiber scaling, which under Z2 is Killing and parallel. (i) The $\Lambda^2$ parts of the odd equation are rational functions of $(t,u)$ times $dt\wedge du$. For $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$, the multiples of $W_A$ and every member with $\ell\neq0$ one of them is not identically zero, so $dt\wedge du=0$ on $U$. (ii) Z2 has no Ricci-special open sets: no nonempty open set carries $\mathrm{Ric}^Y=\phi\,g_\aleph+\psi\,E^\flat\otimes E^\flat$ for functions $\phi,\psi$, or $\mathrm{Ric}^Y=\psi\,\theta\otimes\theta$ with $\theta$ horizontal and null. In particular, for $n=4$ and every $\lambda\neq\tfrac14$, Z2 is Einstein on no open subset of $Y$. By O'Neill's formulas such a set would make the norm $|A|^2$ of O'Neill's integrability tensor constant on a piece of a fiber; polynomial continuation to $y=g(x)$, where $A=0$, makes it vanish, and the remaining conditions are contradictory. (iii) A concircular function on Z2 has parallel gradient, since the fiber $\mathbb R\times SL(4)/SO(p,q)$ has no warped factor.

(iv) The combination $yE^{(1)}-xE^{(13)}$ contains no curvature. Let $\ell\neq0$. Where its second-order coefficient along $(t,u)$ is nonzero, a reparametrisation $r$ of the level function would be concircular with $\mathrm{Ric}^Y\in\mathrm{span}(g_\aleph,dr\otimes dr)$, which (ii) and (iii) exclude. So $(t,u)$ moves along a kernel field, and the traceless part of the combination is then the residual $R'=-1916928\,\kappa^3(x^2\pm y^2)(xz\pm yw)^2(ty-ux)$ times a nonzero tensor. Hence, if also $N\neq0$, $(t,u)\parallel(x,y)$, and this line is invariant under the kernel field only if $(z,w)\parallel(x,y)$; otherwise $(t,u)$ is locally constant, $T_e=0$ and $\mathrm{Ric}^Y\propto g_\aleph$, against (ii). For the multiples of $W_A$ ($\ell=0$) the same channel forces $u\equiv0$ instead. (v) On the invariant line (on $u=0$ for the multiples of $W_A$), a reparametrisation $r$ of the line coordinate satisfies $\nabla^2r=-\tfrac7{18}\,r\,\mathrm{Ric}^Y+\nu g_\aleph$, and the Euler field forces $Er=0$ and $\nu=0$. At a point of $s(X)$ the induced equation on the fiber has no solution: at order $\varepsilon^2$ in the fiber, pairing its integrability condition with one fixed tensor removes the fiber-curvature and $A$-tensor terms and leaves $-360\mu'=0$, here with $\mu'=-\tfrac7{18}$. Analytic continuation along the fiber extends this to open sets that do not meet $s(X)$. Hence $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution on any nonempty open set for every member with $\ell\neq0$ and $N\neq0$ (in $(14,0)$ every elliptic member; in $(7,7)$ every member with $xz-yw\neq0$ and $x\neq\pm y$) and for the multiples of $W_A$ (write-up L; \texttt{L\_nonradial.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round8.out}).

\emph{The remaining members of the family (Z2).} The reduced system is invariant under a rotation (in $(14,0)$) or a boost (in $(7,7)$) acting on $(x,y)$, $(z,w)$ and $(t,u)$ together, and in $(7,7)$ under $(x,y,z,w;t,u)\mapsto(-y,-x,-w,-z;u,t)$ combined with $g_\aleph\to-g_\aleph$, which stays in the setting. Up to these, the members not yet covered are $x(W_A+cW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma)$ with $c\neq0$, those with $x=\pm y$ in $(7,7)$, and $(x,y)=0$. For $x(W_A+cW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma)$ a first-order channel forces $u\equiv0$, and one equation $\nabla^2r=\mu(r)\,\mathrm{Ric}^Y+\nu g_\aleph$ remains. For $c\neq0$, $\mu$ is linear in $r$ only for $c^2=\tfrac{11}6$ in $(14,0)$, where $\mu'\equiv-\tfrac3{289}$ and the argument of (v) excludes every open set. At a point $p\in s(X)$ the fiber obstruction extends to every $\mu$ up to one case: $\mu'(w(p))=0$ and the curvature operator of $g$ at $\pi(p)$ satisfies $\mathcal R^2=-\tfrac1{12}(c_H/c_V)^2\,\mathrm{Id}$ on $\Lambda^2$. Such curvature is not Ricci-flat (for Ricci-flat curvature a Gr\"obner basis $\{1\}$ certifies this) and is real only over a Lorentzian base; there a further step along $s(X)$ forces $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$ and so excludes it. For $x=\pm y$ in $(7,7)$ either $T_e$ vanishes ($\ell=0$) or the curvature-free channel leaves one invariant line, on which the remaining equation is linear, $\mu=-\tfrac5{12}r$. For $(x,y)=0$ the curvature drops out of both channels. Hence, for every member with $(x,y)\neq0$, in $(14,0)$ and $(7,7)$, $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution on any open set meeting $s(X)$. It has none on any nonempty open set at all, except possibly for the members with $\ell=0$, $N\neq0$ and $(z,w)\neq0$ (up to the symmetries $x(W_A+cW'_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma)$ with $c\neq0$, and $c^2\neq\tfrac{11}6$ in $(14,0)$) on open sets that do not meet $s(X)$. For these, exact ranks at sample points and a genericity argument confine a solution to base curvatures in a proper real-algebraic subset, which is not determined. For $(x,y)=0$, $\Upsilon=0$ holds on a connected open set if and only if $T=0$ there, for every $g_\aleph$ (write-up M; \texttt{M\_remaining.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round9.out}).

\emph{The draft-literal metric Z1.} Under Z1 the Euler field is not Killing. It is a gradient with $\nabla E=\tfrac12P_H$ ($P_H$ the horizontal projection), no nonzero conformal Killing field is tangent to the fibers, and still $\mathrm{Ric}(E,\cdot)=-E^\flat/(4c_V(1-4\lambda))$ at every point. Over a flat base, Z1 is Einstein on an open set if and only if $\lambda=\tfrac15$, and then on all of $Y$. Take the same hypotheses as above on a connected open set $U$ that meets $s(X)$. The algebraic parts of steps (i), (iv) and (v) (the $\Lambda^2$ step, the residual $R'$ and the reduction to the line) do not involve the metric and carry over, and for the concircular branch a function $r$ with $\nabla^2r\propto g_\aleph$ and $\mathrm{Ric}^Y\in\mathrm{span}(g_\aleph,dr\otimes dr)$ is constant on Z1. Points $q\in s(X)$ in the closure of the interior of $\{dt=du=0\}$ are Einstein points ($T_e=0$ on that interior), which forces $\lambda=\tfrac15$ and $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$ at $\pi(q)$ as in the theorem above. Near the other points $\Upsilon=0$ is again the line equation $\nabla^2r=-\tfrac7{18}\,r\,\mathrm{Ric}^Y+\nu g_\aleph$, now with $\nu$ fixed by its trace. At $p\in s(X)$ the Ricci identity at first order (3-jets) forces $\nabla r(p)\in\mathbb RE$, an Einstein base at $\pi(p)$, and $(\mathrm{scal}_g/c_H)^2=10(1-5\lambda)(13-56\lambda)/(7c_V^2(1-4\lambda)^2)$ there. Each point $(x,e^\theta g(x))$ of the radial set lies on the section of the parallel metric $e^\theta g$, whose scalar curvature is $e^{-\theta}\mathrm{scal}_g$. So these conditions persist along that set only if $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$ at $\pi(p)$ and $\lambda\in\{\tfrac15,\tfrac{13}{56}\}$, and the trace equation along it excludes $\tfrac{13}{56}$. At $\lambda=\tfrac15$ over a definite base the order-$\varepsilon^2$ condition in the fiber pairs to $\alpha|W|^2$, with $W$ the Weyl tensor of $g$ at $\pi(p)$ and $\alpha=45/(8c_H^2c_V)$, computed exactly at three values of $(c_H,c_V)$ and extended by scaling, so $W=0$ there; over a flat base the line equation has no solution with $dr\neq0$. The other computer-assisted input is the vertical derivative of $\mathrm{Ric}(\mathrm{Z1})$ at the points of $s(X)$, which an affinity argument, Schur's lemma and a degree bound in $\lambda$ reduce to finitely many exact evaluations.

Hence, under Z1, for $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$, the multiples of $W_A$ and every member with $\ell\neq0$ and $N\neq0$: in $(7,7)$, where $\lambda>\tfrac14$, $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution on any connected open set meeting $s(X)$; in $(14,0)$ it forces $\lambda=\tfrac15$ and $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$ on $\pi(U\cap s(X))$, and if $t,u$ are real-analytic (for $xw\neq yz$ without this hypothesis), then $(t,u)$ is constant, $T_e\equiv0$ and $g$ is flat on $\pi(U\cap s(X))$. For smooth fields, limit points of Einstein pieces on $s(X)$ are not excluded, and there only $\mathrm{Ric}(g)=0$ is shown. Conversely, over a flat base of signature $(4,0)$ at $\lambda=\tfrac15$, for $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ with $c_V>0$, solutions of $\Upsilon=0$ on all of $Y$ with constant $(t,u)$ and $T_e\equiv0$ exist if and only if $c_V\kappa^2\ge9360$; their 4D cosmological term vanishes. Finally, for $n=4$ over a definite base, Z1 is Einstein on a neighbourhood of a point $p\in s(X)$ only if $\lambda=\tfrac15$ and $R_g(\pi(p))=0$. So the invariant-sector solutions with $T_e\equiv0$ in $(14,0)$ near a point $p\in s(X)$, described above, also need $R_g(\pi(p))=0$ (write-up N; \texttt{N\_z1\_neighbourhood.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round10.out}).

\emph{The variation of the base metric.} The draft treats the base metric $g$ (its $\gimel$) as a field. A compactly supported variation of $g$ on $X$ is not compactly supported on $Y$, so the $\gimel$-equation needs a weight $\nu=(\nu_x)$ on the fibers. Call it natural if $\nu_x$ is obtained from $g_x$ by a rule that commutes with linear isomorphisms. At $T=0$ the action density vanishes and the $\gimel$-equation is empty; at first order in $T$ it is the weighted Einstein--Hilbert equation of $g_\aleph$. With the fiber-invariant measure, or with the volume of $g_\aleph$ itself, the fiber integral of the invariant-sector density with constant odd part and $T_e=0$ diverges unless the density vanishes on the fiber. For an indefinite base, that is in every $(7,7)$ case, a signed measure of finite total variation on the fiber that is invariant under the identity component of $O(g_x)$ is concentrated on the multiples of $g_x$: under a boost, mass off that set would escape to infinity, which Poincar\'e recurrence excludes. Hence no nonzero natural weight function is integrable on the fiber, and the natural weights for which the invariant-sector action converges for every base and every constant odd part are exactly the measures $\mu$ on the multiples $\pm e^\tau g_x$ (the scaling ray through $s(X)$, and for a base $(2,2)$ also its negative) with $\mu$ finite under Z2 and $\int(e^\tau+e^{2\tau})\,d|\mu|<\infty$ under Z1. In $(14,0)$ a large class of smooth natural weights converges; under Z2 a positive one adds to the equation below a positive multiple of the Euler--Lagrange tensor of $\int|\mathrm{Ric}_0|^2$, which vanishes on Einstein metrics. Now take the invariant sector $T=t\Phi^1+u\Phi^1\Gamma$ with constant $t,u$, $T_e=0$ and $xt\pm yu\neq0$, vary $g$ at fixed $(t,u)$, and keep $\Upsilon=0$ unweighted, as the draft writes it. For a ray weight whose Einstein coefficient $m_1$, a moment of $\mu$, is nonzero, the $\gimel$-equation is exactly $G(g)+\Lambda g=0$, with
\[ \Lambda_O=\Lambda_A-c_H(t^2\pm u^2)\Bigl(\frac{7\kappa}{12(xt\pm yu)}+\frac{364}3\Bigr),\qquad \Lambda=\Lambda_O\ \text{(Z2, for every such }\mu),\qquad \Lambda=r_\mu\Lambda_O\ \text{(Z1)}. \]
Here $\Lambda_A$ is the reduced-action value of the theorem above ($9c_H/(2c_V)$ under Z2, $\tfrac{5c_H}{4c_V}\tfrac{5-18\lambda}{1-4\lambda}$ under Z1), and $r_\mu=m_2/m_1$ is a ratio of moments of $\mu$. So as $T\to0$ the $\gimel$-variation selects the reduced-action value, not the projected one: under Z1, for every such weight, the limit $r_\mu\Lambda_A$ is a $\lambda$-independent multiple of $\Lambda_A$ (equal to $\Lambda_A$ for the weight concentrated at $s(X)$), and it vanishes at $\lambda=\tfrac5{18}$, not at the zero $\lambda=\tfrac4{15}$ of the projected value. This is a statement about the $\gimel$-equation alone, off-shell. In this sector $\Upsilon=0$ holds near $s(X)$ only under Z1 in $(14,0)$ over a flat base, by (ii), the theorem above and the previous paragraph. For $W_A$ and $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ these flat-base solutions also satisfy the $\gimel$-equation, for every admissible weight, exactly when $c_V\kappa^2=12480$ (and at one of the two values of $t$); in $(7,7)$ no joint solution is known. If the weight is used also in the equation for the fields on $Y$, it forces $xt\pm yu=0$ (on the scaling ray, for ray weights), and the invariant sector then gives no 4D gravitational equation. Under Z2, beyond leading order in $T$, the off-shell equation also depends on how the configuration spaces for different $g$ are identified, which the draft does not fix (write-up O; \texttt{O\_gimel.out}, \texttt{xcheck\_round11.out}).

\section{Summary}
The finite-dimensional algebra of \cite{W21} is largely correct and is reproducible exactly. The parts that carry physical content are each well-posed only after explicit choices the draft does not fix:
\begin{itemize}
\item the parameter $\lambda$ and the block sign;
\item the space $Y$: the $\mathrm{Spin}(7,7)$ bundle exists only over the time-oriented double cover, and there only if $w_2(X)=0$;
\item the tilt sign, which comes with a matching projection;
\item the Shiab operator;
\item the observerse metric, Z1 or Z2;
\item the rule that fixes $T$ off the section, including its second normal derivatives;
\item the fiber weight for the variation of the base metric, the system of equations it enters, and the identification of the configuration spaces for different base metrics;
\item the reading of the 4D equations (projected, reduced action, or $\Upsilon=0$ at or near the section).
\end{itemize}
One sign error is established (\S4; \texttt{GUTilt.lean}). The field equation is non-variational for every nonzero admissible Shiab operator, provided $\varpi$ is varied at fixed $\varepsilon$, the operator does not depend on $\varpi$, and the pairing is constant and invariant. The single-Majorana--Weyl fermion reading is inconsistent. The claimed elliptic deformation complex fails for the draft's own Shiab form, though it holds for generic self-adjoint Shiab operators in $(14,0)$, with gauge algebra $\mathfrak u(128)$ and $\kappa\neq0$; in $(7,7)$ it fails for all of them. In the invariant odd sector with the even part slaved ($\kappa\neq0$, $\det M\neq0$), $\Upsilon=0$ has no solution near the section under Z2 for any member with $(x,y)\neq0$. Under Z1, for $W_A$, $W_A+W_C\circ\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma$ and the members with $xz\pm yw\neq0$ and $x^2\pm y^2\neq0$, it has none near the section in $(7,7)$, and in $(14,0)$ its real-analytic solutions near the section have a flat base and no 4D cosmological term. On the invariant sector the equation for the base metric is 4D vacuum GR, with a cosmological term that tends to the reduced-action value as $T\to0$ (under Z1 up to a factor set by the weight), but only off-shell and for a fiber weight that the draft does not specify; in $(7,7)$ naturality confines that weight to the multiples of the base metric. No prediction yet has a mass scale.
